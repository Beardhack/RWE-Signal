# The AI never saw a patient row

**RWE-Signal - August 8, 2026 - Daily edition**

A new agentic analytics design kept patient records inside local R, while three extraction studies showed how external validation, missing source types, and scanned-document errors can still weaken AI-derived evidence.

## The cloud model wrote the plan; local R touched the data

- **Source:** [Yang et al., medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.30.26359367v1)
- **Date:** August 2, 2026
- **What happened:** An agentic system translated natural-language research requests into locally executed R workflows and reproduced a published prognostic-model analysis without sending row-level patient data to the external language model.
- **Why it matters:** Separating remote reasoning from local computation reduces one important disclosure path and leaves executable code for review.
- **Evidence limits:** One preprint and one reproduced study; no broad statistical-validity or multicenter usability benchmark. Data locality does not prove that the selected methods are correct or that every privacy obligation is satisfied.

## External validation cut exact lupus scores by 22 points

- **Source:** [Zhang et al., medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.09.26357586v1)
- **Date:** July 10, 2026
- **What happened:** A privacy-preserving LLM workflow reconstructed SLEDAI-2K disease-activity scores from EHR notes. Exact agreement was 86% internally and 64% on 56 external MIMIC-IV summaries.
- **Why it matters:** Small descriptor errors compounded inside the composite endpoint, making local validation insufficient for downstream cohort use.
- **Evidence limits:** Preprint; small, mismatched external set; retrospective outcome associations in 108 patients were not causal estimates.

## Diagnosis and prognosis required different source data

- **Source:** [Khan et al., medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.06.26.26356633v1.full)
- **Date:** July 9, 2026
- **What happened:** An LLM-based framework combined pathology, notes, and structured labs to phenotype three rare blood cancers. Pathology alone supported strong diagnosis, but prognostic accuracy fell to 69.3%-93.3% until notes were added.
- **Why it matters:** A source can be fit for cohort identification while remaining incomplete for risk stratification.
- **Evidence limits:** Single-site preprint with unusually high held-out performance, only 172 patients with notes, and no prospective validation.

## Scanned records added a failure stage before the prompt

- **Source:** [Shrestha et al., Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-58657-x)
- **Date:** July 12, 2026
- **What happened:** A two-stage workflow used computer vision and ChatGPT-3.5 to extract variables from image-based sleep-study reports. Numerical, symbol, and encoding errors appeared across both stages.
- **Why it matters:** The reliability of an AI-derived variable depends on the entire image-to-text-to-value chain, not the language model alone.
- **Evidence limits:** One hospital and document environment; detailed quantitative tables were not exposed in the accessible early publisher page.

## What changed

Privacy, accuracy, and evidence fitness separated into distinct validation problems. Keeping rows local is meaningful, but credible RWE still requires review of statistical choices, external phenotype performance, source completeness, and upstream document conversion.

## Source health

Europe PMC, PubMed, FDA, ISPOR, and institutional pages were accessible. Direct medRxiv article routes returned HTTP 403, the current Scientific Reports page did not expose detailed result tables, and public LinkedIn indexing remained incomplete. Alternate primary records were used for verification; social indexing was used only for discovery.

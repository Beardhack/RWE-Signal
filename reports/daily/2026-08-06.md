# The same patient data produced different disability scores

**RWE-Signal — August 6, 2026 — Daily edition**

Two multiple-sclerosis preprints show that an LLM-derived RWE phenotype can drift both inside the model and in the endpoint logic that follows it.

## Endpoint logic changed scores after extraction was fixed

- **Source:** [Hwang et al., medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.06.25.26356350v1.full)
- **Date:** July 7, 2026
- **Topic:** Specification drift in an LLM-supported clinical endpoint
- **What happened:** Researchers held eight LLM-derived functional-system subscores fixed across 57,604 notes, then computed the Expanded Disability Status Scale with a natural-language prompt, LLM-generated deterministic code, and a canonical open-source implementation.
- **Why it matters:** The deterministic calculators matched exactly on 98.1% of notes. Exact agreement fell to 84.2% and 82.8% when the natural-language calculator was compared with those implementations. Most disagreements persisted across repeated calls, showing that re-prompting does not correct a systematic specification difference.
- **Evidence limits:** This is a preprint from one disease registry. The shared subscores were model-predicted rather than clinician ground truth, the canonical implementation also contains interpretation choices, and the paper did not test downstream treatment-effect estimates.
- **Practical follow-up:** Which endpoint rules, branch ordering, and boundary cases are versioned and tested before an AI-derived variable enters cohorting or outcomes analysis?

## A clinical decision tree constrained GPT-4, but did not eliminate hallucinations

- **Source:** [Venkatesh et al., medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.14.26357416v1.full)
- **Date:** July 17, 2026
- **Topic:** Structured LLM phenotyping from neurology notes
- **What happened:** GPT-4 traversed a decision tree based on the 2017 McDonald criteria to flag multiple-sclerosis diagnoses from the first neurology note of 125 clinic patients.
- **Why it matters:** Accuracy was 84%, precision 79%, recall 74%, and specificity 91%, but hallucinations appeared in 32 cases. Explicit structure makes errors easier to locate; it does not make the resulting cohort label self-validating.
- **Evidence limits:** The preprint used a small, highly enriched clinic sample and had no external or prospective validation. It tested diagnostic flagging, not the downstream validity of an RWE study.
- **Practical follow-up:** Before reuse as a phenotype, how do error rates change at real-world prevalence, across sites, and at the cohort boundaries that matter to a study?

## What changed

Validation has to cover both sides of the handoff: the model that extracts or reasons over clinical evidence, and the deterministic or prompt-based rules that turn those outputs into research variables.

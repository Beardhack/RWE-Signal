# A pan-cancer model carried real-world learning across hospitals

**RWE-Signal - August 19, 2026 - Daily edition**

Two new preprints connect foundation models to real-world evidence workflows: transferable clinico-genomic outcome modeling across institutions and retrieval-augmented MedDRA coding across three prospective AML trials.

## PanoraOnc learned from 84,131 real-world cancer patients

- **Source:** [Schürch et al., medRxiv v1](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.17.26354679v1)
- **Date:** August 18, 2026
- **What happened:** A Gemma3-12B-based model was pretrained on 84,131 patients across Dana-Farber, Memorial Sloan Kettering, and a melanoma whole-exome/RNA cohort. It transferred outcome predictions across institutions, cancer types, and treatment settings without cohort-specific training in the zero-shot tests.
- **Why it matters:** The work treats broad real-world clinico-genomic data as reusable model pretraining rather than rebuilding an outcome model from zero for every small local cohort.
- **Evidence limits:** This is a preprint. Endpoints differ across cohorts, the counterfactual analysis retains observational assumptions, and proposed biomarkers and the interactive agent require prospective validation.

## A local RAG pipeline mapped trial adverse events into MedDRA

- **Source:** [Dashti et al., medRxiv v1](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.17.26360282v1)
- **Date:** August 18, 2026
- **What happened:** The pipeline retrieved candidate MedDRA terms, used a locally run Llama-3.3-70B model to select a Low Level Term, and deterministically mapped it to Preferred Term and System Organ Class across 848 unique adverse-event descriptions from three controlled AML trials.
- **Why it matters:** The system combines model flexibility with a traceable terminology hierarchy and clinician review, a practical pattern for AI-assisted safety-data standardization.
- **Evidence limits:** This is a retrospective AML-only preprint. The study did not yet measure prospective coding time, user acceptance, formal regulatory validation, or transport to other specialties, languages, and MedDRA versions.

## What changed

Foundation-model capability is entering two complementary evidence layers. PanoraOnc packages learning from large, heterogeneous real-world oncology cohorts for transfer to new settings. The MedDRA pipeline packages semantic interpretation as a controlled terminology decision that remains traceable through the safety hierarchy.

The AI Never Saw a Patient Row

An agentic analytics system kept records local, while three studies exposed the failures that can still enter between a chart and an evidence variable

August 8, 2026 — Daily edition

A cloud language model planned a clinical analysis, generated the R workflow, and helped refine the research question. It never received the patient table.

That separation is the central design choice in a new agentic clinical-research preprint. The model reasoned remotely; deterministic R code executed beside the data. In a functional test, the system reproduced a published prognostic-model analysis through natural-language dialogue without manual programming or row-level data leaving the local environment.

Three other studies published within the past month show why locality is only one layer of trustworthy AI-derived RWE. A lupus phenotype lost 22 percentage points of exact-score agreement outside its development setting. A rare-cancer pipeline could diagnose from pathology alone but needed clinical notes for prognosis. A sleep-study workflow inherited numerical and encoding errors from scanned reports before the language model extracted a value.

Together, the evidence moves validation away from a single model score. The data boundary, statistical specification, external record environment, source completeness, and document-conversion chain each need a separate test.

The model wrote the analysis. Local R touched the records.

Shujie Yang and colleagues reported the agentic system on August 2. An n8n orchestrator connected DeepSeek-Reasoner to a retrieval layer grounded in curated biostatistical guidance and R templates. The external model could inspect the schema, clarify the request, select a workflow, and generate code. The patient-level computation occurred locally.

The system reproduced a published prognostic-model study in metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease. Through dialogue alone, it performed cleaning, multivariable Cox regression, model diagnostics, and publication-oriented outputs. The authors say the workflow reduced an analysis that took days to a process measured in hours.

The claim is architectural and functional, not yet general. The preprint replicated one prognostic study and did not run a preregistered battery of estimands, missing-data strategies, or causal designs. It did not establish that a cloud-generated analysis plan is statistically sound merely because the code executes locally. Data locality is a privacy control; it is not a methods validator.

A composite disease score traveled worse than its descriptors

Danting Zhang and colleagues reported on July 10 a privacy-preserving workflow for reconstructing the SLEDAI-2K lupus disease-activity score from real EHR notes. Seven open-source language models were tested against 658 specialist-verified notes, then externally against 56 MIMIC-IV discharge summaries.

The top configuration paired GPT-OSS-120B with a verifier. Internal descriptor classification reached a 94.2% micro-F1, while exact agreement on the final composite score was 86%. On the external summaries, those measures fell to 87.7% and 64%.

The larger decline at the score level matters for evidence generation. A composite endpoint can magnify small descriptor mistakes when the errors change weighted components or cross a threshold. A phenotype that looks stable by component-level F1 may still move patients between disease-activity groups.

The authors applied the model to 2,576 serial notes from 108 patients. Sustained low disease activity identified by the LLM was associated with less stage 3 chronic kidney disease, kidney replacement therapy, and hospitalization over 18.3 years. Those retrospective associations support face validity, but they are not causal effects. The preprint's external test was small and used a different note type; site-specific validation remains necessary before the labels become comparative-effectiveness variables.

Pathology could identify the disease. It could not finish the prognosis.

Muhammad Ali Khan and colleagues built an LLM-based phenotyping pipeline for polycythemia vera, essential thrombocythemia, and myelofibrosis. GPT-4 Turbo extracted diagnostic and prognostic variables; deterministic WHO, ICC, and NCCN-aligned rules produced classifications and risk groups.

The July 9 preprint used 60 patients for prompt development and a balanced held-out test set of 450. With pathology reports, clinical notes, and structured labs together, the authors reported near-perfect diagnosis and 100% risk-stratification accuracy across all three diseases. A prespecified source-ablation test revealed what the aggregate number hid.

Pathology alone still supported high diagnostic sensitivity: 98.7% for polycythemia vera, 100% for essential thrombocythemia, and 96% for myelofibrosis. But prognostic accuracy fell to 69.3%, 93.3%, and 77.3%. Adding clinical notes restored the reported risk accuracy to 100%.

The distinction is consequential for RWE design. A dataset can be fit for identifying a disease cohort while remaining unfit for stratifying baseline risk or comparing outcomes. The unusually high results also demand caution: this was a single-site preprint, only 172 held-out patients had notes, and the balanced test set did not reproduce disease prevalence. Prospective multisite validation is the next test.

The scanned chart failed before the language model did

Bikal Shrestha and colleagues addressed a less fashionable but common evidence problem: clinical reports stored as images. Their peer-reviewed Scientific Reports study, published July 12, processed polysomnography reports from a tertiary-care sleep laboratory in Thailand.

The pipeline first used DBNet within EasyOCR to turn scanned report images into text. ChatGPT-3.5 then used task-specific prompts to extract sleep-study variables. Prompt refinement reduced errors, but the failure analysis found recurring numerical, symbol, and character-encoding mistakes across the conversion and extraction stages.

The implication is procedural. A language model can faithfully structure a number that OCR already corrupted. Validation therefore needs page-level provenance and separate error accounting for image recognition and value extraction, especially for decimals, units, and symbols that drive cohort or endpoint definitions.

The study came from one hospital and document environment. The accessible publisher page currently carries an early unedited-manuscript notice and exposes the abstract rather than the detailed result tables. The authors themselves call for testing across document qualities and conditions before the workflow is generalized.

What changed: validation followed the whole evidence chain

Today's signal is architectural. Privacy, statistical correctness, phenotype transport, source completeness, and document conversion are distinct controls. Improving one does not silently satisfy the others.

The strongest practical pattern is a divided workflow: keep patient-level computation local, preserve the generated code, validate component labels and final endpoints externally, test which source types each research variable requires, and trace extracted values back through OCR to the original page.

That is a less dramatic claim than autonomous evidence generation. It is also a more credible one. The model can accelerate the work while the study remains a chain of reviewable, versioned decisions.

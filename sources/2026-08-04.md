# Research notes — 2026-08-04

## Editorial line

**Headline:** Two architectures put guardrails around AI for RWE

Two qualifying papers give models bounded, inspectable roles inside the RWE lifecycle. EmulatRx distributes work across a supervised agent team; THESEUS uses an LLM to build a structured specification before human review and deterministic compilation.

## Ranked evidence

### 1. Li et al. — EmulatRx

- Direct sources: [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-74501-2), [complete text in PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13341881/)
- Publication date: July 7, 2026
- DOI: `10.1038/s41467-026-74501-2`
- Topic: Multi-agent AI across the clinical-trial emulation and RWE lifecycle
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. The primary paper describes an agentic system whose named tasks include trial retrieval, EHR cohort construction, eligibility parsing, clinical interpretation, causal analysis, and RWE synthesis.
- What the source establishes:
  - A Supervisor coordinates Trialist, Informatician, Clinician, and Statistician agents.
  - The evaluation covers 20 trials: ten acute-care trials using MIMIC-IV and ten chronic-disease trials using INSIGHT.
  - On 266 manually annotated eligibility concepts, GPT-4o reached 98.9% recall and 96.7% precision.
  - In one emulation, NLP over notes found 33 additional eligible patients not discoverable from structured EHR fields alone.
  - The system records workflow state and transformations, caches model responses, and fixes random seeds for downstream tools.
- Why it matters: it makes the evidence lifecycle, including agent handoffs and stored intermediate state, the object of the system rather than treating RWE as an output-writing task.
- Limits: some causal evaluations used synthetic cohorts; clinician assessment involved three experts; the trial set was small; task-level performance does not establish end-to-end causal or regulatory validity.
- Follow-up question: which handoffs can change population, exposure, outcome, estimand, or adjustment choices, and can an independent reviewer reconstruct each change?

### 2. Kim et al. — THESEUS

- Direct source: [JAMIA Open](https://academic.oup.com/jamiaopen/article/9/4/ooag131/8729191)
- Publication date: July 9, 2026
- DOI: `10.1093/jamiaopen/ooag131`
- Topic: LLM-assisted target-trial specification and deterministic R code generation
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. The primary paper evaluates eight LLMs inside a target-trial-emulation workflow designed to generate executable observational RWE analyses.
- What the source establishes:
  - Natural-language trial descriptions are mapped to JSON, reviewed field by field, and compiled by deterministic rules into Strategus R code.
  - The evaluation includes 15 OHDSI and 15 non-OHDSI studies across eight LLMs.
  - Primary-analysis specification accuracy ranged from 0.93 to 0.97 for OHDSI studies and 0.82 to 0.95 for non-OHDSI studies.
  - The supported specification groups are study period, time at risk, propensity-score adjustment, and outcome modeling.
- Why it matters: it confines model discretion to a reviewable language-interpretation step and keeps executable code generation deterministic.
- Limits: cohort definition is outside scope; unsupported analytic choices are excluded; the study did not establish analyst time savings, completed-study validity, or regulatory fitness; the OHDSI/non-OHDSI gap raises transportability questions.
- Follow-up question: which field-level specification errors are most consequential for the estimand, and how stable is performance across institutions and reporting conventions?

## What changed

The two qualifying sources do not support autonomous RWE generation. They support narrower architectural claims: specialize model roles, preserve state, expose intermediate artifacts, retain human review, and use deterministic tools where execution can be specified in advance.

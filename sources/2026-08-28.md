## Ranked evidence

### 1. HealthFlow: automating electronic health record analysis via a strategically self-evolving multi-agent framework

- Direct sources: [npj Digital Medicine version of record](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03097-0) and [open code and benchmark release](https://github.com/yhzhu99/HealthFlow)
- Publication date: August 17, 2026
- Stable ID: `doi:10.1038/s41746-026-03097-0`; story ID: `healthflow-self-evolving-multi-agent-ehr-analysis-2026`; content version: `version-of-record`
- Internal score: 10/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes as a multi-agent framework that performs end-to-end analytical work on EHR datasets**, including cohort construction, phenotyping, temporal prediction, feature engineering, modeling, visualization, and reporting.
- What the source establishes: the four-agent loop separates planning, execution, evaluation/repair, and governed reflection. EHRFlowBench contains 100 tasks across 10 EHR-analysis categories, derived from 118 peer-reviewed seed papers after screening 51,280 papers, and is instantiated on TJH and the MIMIC-IV Public Demo Release. HealthFlow scored 4.01 versus 3.76 for Biomni on EHRFlowBench and 63.05% versus 50.10% on MedAgentBoard. Twelve domain experts made 240 blinded preferences across 20 reports; HealthFlow led most often.
- Limits: EHRFlowBench uses reference-guided LLM judging, although the authors report agreement checks against stronger judges and experts. The MIMIC input is the public demo rather than the full dataset. The reported hospital pilot is qualitative and offline, not a controlled prospective evaluation. Benchmark success is not yet proof that autonomous analyses are clinically or regulatorily valid.
- Novelty: the 2026 peer-reviewed version of record and public code/benchmark release were not previously published by RWE-Signal; the 2025 arXiv precursor was never covered.

### 2. Extracting adverse event nature, severity, timelines and resulting interventions from CAR-T clinical notes using an LLM

- Direct sources: [PLOS Digital Health DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pdig.0001426), [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42658846/), and [open full research artifact](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13174767/)
- Publication date: August 27, 2026 (peer-reviewed version of record; earlier preprint posted May 5)
- Stable ID: `doi:10.1371/journal.pdig.0001426`; story ID: `llm-car-t-rwd-adverse-event-extraction-2026`; content version: `version-of-record`
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes as a GPT-4 workflow converting longitudinal EHR notes into structured real-world safety evidence** for pharmacovigilance and CAR-T adverse-event characterization.
- What the source establishes: GPT-4-0314 processed 4,762 progress notes from 293 UCSF patients treated with six commercial CAR-T products from 2012-2023. The source reports CRS in 80.2% of patients with median onset at day 4, neutropenia in 70.0% with median onset at day 16, neutropenic fever in 64.8% with median onset at day 4, and ICANS in 34.8%. Grade extraction accuracy was 0.97 for CRS and 0.91 for ICANS; broader adverse-event attribute accuracy ranged from 0.62 to 0.84 depending on prompt design.
- Limits: dual human validation covered 50 notes from 47 patients at one center; broader attribute agreement was weaker than focused CRS/ICANS extraction. Clinical grades were often not documented. The study used an older model snapshot and did not provide prospective or external-site validation.
- Novelty: the peer-reviewed PLOS version of record is new and materially distinct from the May preprint record.

### 3. Performance of locally deployed LLMs in extracting intestinal symptoms from EHRs

- Direct source: [peer-reviewed JMIR article](https://www.jmir.org/2026/1/e98580)
- Publication date: August 26, 2026
- Stable ID: `doi:10.2196/98580`; story ID: `local-llm-intestinal-symptom-rwd-surveillance-2026`; content version: `version-of-record`
- Internal score: 8/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes as a locally deployed LLM workflow that structures chief-complaint fields from a citywide real-world outpatient data platform for syndromic surveillance**.
- What the source establishes: the underlying Wuhan platform contained 167,567,887 deidentified outpatient records from 2023-2025. Twelve open-source LLMs from four families and three prompting strategies were evaluated on 1,000 randomly selected chief complaints, with 50 separate development examples. Qwen3-8B reached macro F1 0.89, recall 0.91, precision 0.88, and 3.64 seconds per record; Qwen3-1.7B reached macro F1 0.85. Human inter-rater kappa across the six symptom labels averaged 0.92.
- Limits: the experiment used a 1,000-record sample from one Chinese city and one short EHR field, not the full 167.6 million records. Rare symptoms such as nausea remained more difficult. The work was retrospective, not a prospective surveillance deployment, and did not include a dedicated hallucination metric.

## Friday synthesis

Across the August 22-28 editions, the durable pattern was not simply larger models. Strong releases bounded AI around inspectable evidence objects: DrugEvolve and the Brain Researcher kit preserved lineage and deterministic checks; FDA's discussion paper tied generative-AI oversight to lifecycle monitoring; TrialCode, CLARA, oFM, and the new extraction studies turned source records into structured representations; HealthFlow added evaluation, repair, and governed memory across whole analytical workflows. The tension also repeated: explicit, prevalent, well-scoped targets performed best, while rare, broad, ambiguous, or undocumented attributes remained the harder frontier. Human review increasingly functions as part of the system architecture rather than a final disclaimer.

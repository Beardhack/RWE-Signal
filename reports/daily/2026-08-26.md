# LLMs turn clinical notes into future queries and structured evidence

**RWE-Signal - August 26, 2026 - Daily edition**

Three August 24 benchmarks advance the unstructured-record pipeline from longitudinal patient-outcome questions to SNOMED-coded extraction and evidence-bounded terminology linking. Together, they make the AI-plus-RWE architecture more modular, measurable, and ready for explicit human checkpoints.

## A medical world model learned to query what happened next

- **Source:** [Future Querying: Can LLMs Serve as Implicit Medical World Models?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23248)
- **Date:** August 24, 2026
- **What changed:** The framework turns longitudinal patient notes into time-indexed questions whose answers are checked against later events in the same record. Its MIMIC-IV benchmark contains 9,733 ICU patients and 164,911 questions.
- **Why it matters:** One locally trainable text model can support many outcome questions without building a separate structured pipeline for each endpoint. LoRA fine-tuning improved the tested small models by 11.7 to 21.5 percentage points on MIMIC.
- **Evidence limits:** The arXiv v1 is a preprint accepted to a MICCAI workshop. Evaluation uses an LLM judge with a manually checked subset; clinical-expert validation, a human upper bound, uncertainty estimation, and prospective testing remain open.

## A seven-model benchmark mapped ENT notes to SNOMED

- **Source:** [Developing an open-source framework for LLM evaluation of patients using EHR clinical documentation](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.21.26361031)
- **Date:** August 24, 2026
- **What changed:** Seven LLMs and 14 medical professionals extracted seven categories of SNOMED-coded information from 98 public ENT clinical documents.
- **Why it matters:** The benchmark measures models against observed human disagreement and supports a practical first-pass extraction role. GPT-4o reached 97.0% precision and 84.9% recall.
- **Evidence limits:** Clinician-to-clinician kappa was 0.752 versus 0.391 between LLMs and clinicians, and non-inferiority was not established. The preprint's public ENT dataset is small and narrow.

## Evidence-bounded terminology linking separated the pipeline

- **Source:** [Evaluating Clinical Concept Extraction and Evidence-Bounded Terminology Linking](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.20.26360740)
- **Date:** August 24, 2026
- **What changed:** A three-part pilot evaluated extraction, controlled terminology retrieval, link decisions, and ontology-extension triage as separate stages across de-identified notes from three health systems.
- **Why it matters:** Complete retrieval returned the reference-matched concept for all 28 known-concept spans. Keeping retrieval evidence and model decisions separate creates a clearer audit trail for RWE data engineering.
- **Evidence limits:** The evaluations are small and use different units of analysis. The 84-term ontology-extension follow-up was not independently adjudicated.

## The signal

The new signal is a composable evidence layer: longitudinal outcome queries, coded fact extraction, and terminology linking can be evaluated independently and joined through traceable checkpoints. The next test is whether this architecture remains dependable across institutions, documentation styles, patient populations, and evidence uses.

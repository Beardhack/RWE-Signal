## Ranked evidence

### 1. Bringing analytic rigor to agentic AI for science: The Brain Researcher platform for neuroimaging data analysis

- Direct sources: [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19902), [experimental HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19902v1), [open agent kit v0.3.0](https://github.com/brain-researcher/brain-researcher-agent-kit/tree/v0.3.0), [archived software release](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21937526), and [project page](https://brain-researcher.com/)
- Publication date: August 20, 2026
- arXiv ID: `2608.19902`
- Stable ID: `arxiv:2608.19902`
- Content version: `v1`
- Authors: Zijiao Chen et al.
- Internal score: 10/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes through agentic scientific evidence infrastructure**. A researcher-governed agent harness performs concrete evidence-lifecycle work on open and collaborator-supplied neuroimaging cohorts: tool and dataset routing, prospectively constrained analysis, multiverse evaluation, provenance capture, and claim adjudication.
- What the primary sources establish:
  - The platform combines a machine-readable tool registry, a provenance-linked knowledge graph, an MCP server, version-pinned executors, rule checks, commitment cards, claim cards, and an audit bundle.
  - The July 7 graph snapshot contained 745,949 nodes and 2,461,469 edges. The public project page reports 1,600+ datasets, 2,000+ tool specifications, 87 MCP tools, and 60+ workflows.
  - Across 60 tool-calling tasks and seven frontier models, first-action correct route/tool selection increased from 23.3% without the harness to 93.6% with it; Capability@1 rose from 49.8% to 94.5%, and handoff sufficiency from 47.4% to 76.1%.
  - In a separate 50-question evidence benchmark, verified groundedness rose from 4.6% to 22.0%. Most evidence rows still did not clear the verification threshold; the improvement is substantial but grounding remains an open engineering frontier.
  - Collaborator-led cases included an FBIRN schizophrenia cohort (`N=363`) expanded into a 480-specification multiverse; the SUDMEX OpenNeuro cocaine-use-disorder cohort (`N=138`) evaluated across 36 specifications; and a cross-cultural social-cognition meta-analysis of 21 studies.
  - The SUDMEX workflow rejected five prespecified connectivity-behavior claims and prevented an exploratory 70-combination screen with no FDR-surviving result from being promoted as confirmatory. The cross-cultural case withheld a settled claim because its subgroups had only 6-8 entries, below the authors' cited recommended minimum of 17.
  - The HCP self-evolving episode evaluated 116 candidate prediction pipelines, froze a selected workflow after audit, and then carried it to matched evaluation and four additional outcomes. A TRIBE episode froze a prospectively testable speech-tools contrast and evaluated three non-overlapping 48-item panels plus four previously unused collections.
  - Every evaluated claim receives one of six states: accepted, qualified, revised, blocked, rejected, or deferred. Researchers approve the action space, freeze successor analyses, interpret evidence, and retain final authority.
  - The MIT-licensed public agent kit exposes agent instructions, MCP adapters, sanitized demos, deterministic review kernels, evaluation rubrics, redaction checks, and workflow skills. Zenodo archives v0.3.0 with DOI `10.5281/zenodo.21937526`.
- Why it matters: the artifact operationalizes precommitment, analytic alternatives, provenance, and bounded claim language as part of the agent runtime. That makes a completed episode reviewable as an evidence object and supplies a reusable design pattern for agentic RWE work.
- Evidence limits: this is a 103-page preprint, not peer review. The paired routing comparison bundles the registry, graph, and constraint layer, so it does not isolate one component; target curation may share vocabulary with the registry. Verified grounding reached 22.0%, leaving substantial room for improvement. A human reviewer, not the automated layer, caught a sign-blind scoring error in the NeuroMark case. The self-evolving comparison to unconstrained coding-agent sessions was descriptive rather than matched or causal.

### 2. Testing the use of local large language models to extract trauma identification and contextualize posttraumatic stress symptoms from self-report

- Direct source: [primary PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42631898/)
- Publisher DOI: [10.1007/s10865-026-00707-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10865-026-00707-w)
- Publication date: August 22, 2026
- PMID: `42631898`
- Stable ID: `doi:10.1007/s10865-026-00707-w`
- Content version: `version-of-record`
- Authors: Mikael Rubin, E. Stuart, E. Santos, M. Cordova, and K. Watters
- Internal score: 7/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes as observational behavioral-health phenotyping**. A locally executed LLM converts participant-written trauma narratives into structured exposure types and relates those features to PTSD symptom measurements, a concrete evidence-generation task on observed human health data.
- What the primary record establishes:
  - The study included 109 participants recruited online through Prolific as part of a larger study.
  - Participants completed an extended Life Events Checklist with up to three free-text trauma descriptions and the PTSD Checklist for DSM-5.
  - A local, privacy-preserving LLM extracted trauma types, which were compared with classifications by two expert clinical psychologists.
  - Specificity exceeded 85% for most trauma types. Agreement was strongest for frequently reported experiences; sexual assault reached kappa 0.68-0.74.
  - Cluster analysis using LLM-derived features found differences in total PCL-5 scores across clusters (`p=.04`).
- Why it matters: the system keeps sensitive narrative data local while turning free text into analyzable phenotypes, offering a practical route to augment behavioral-health evidence workflows without a cloud inference path.
- Evidence limits: this is a preliminary `N=109` online self-report study, not routine-care RWD or a clinical deployment. Agreement varied by trauma type, the primary abstract does not name the local model, and association with symptom clusters does not establish screening benefit or clinical utility. The complete Springer article was not publicly accessible during this run; reporting relies on the primary PubMed abstract and metadata.

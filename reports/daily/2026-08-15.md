# A sleep co-scientist put 50 terabytes of physiology to work.

**RWE-Signal - August 15, 2026 - Daily edition**

Three new systems show agentic AI becoming governed evidence infrastructure: one for cohort-scale scientific discovery, one for pharmacovigilance from clinical notes, and one for local longitudinal-data preparation.

## AI Sleep Co-Scientist connected agents to four real-world cohorts

- **Source:** [Thapa et al., arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25175)
- **Date:** July 29, 2026
- **What happened:** Human-directed specialist agents developed hypotheses, built physiological features, ran analyses, and produced code-grounded reports across approximately 124,000 sleep recordings and more than 50 TB of raw signals.
- **Why it matters:** The platform produced five cohort studies, including replicated disease-risk and sleep-age analyses, while binding reported numbers to executable scripts and preserving expert authority at irreversible steps.
- **Evidence limits:** This is a retrospective preprint. Its associations are hypothesis-generating rather than causal, and the derived measures have not been tested as clinical decision tools.

## RAG created an extensible adverse-event detection layer

- **Source:** [Ma et al., JMIR](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13456140/)
- **Date:** August 10, 2026
- **What happened:** Three LLMs were evaluated with nonaugmented, static-augmented, and retrieval-augmented prompts over deidentified Chinese clinical notes. The authors released a 2,510-record reference set and 5,144-record ADE knowledge base.
- **Why it matters:** In low-prevalence real-world notes, DeepSeek-V3 with RAG reached 94.48% recall and 98.21% specificity. The retrieval layer can accept new safety examples without retraining the core model.
- **Evidence limits:** Multicenter external validation is still needed; the study used one overall source distribution and did not compare directly with conventional clinical NLP baselines.

## Open-weight agents brought cohort preparation onto local hardware

- **Source:** [Nixon et al., arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21482)
- **Date:** July 24, 2026
- **What happened:** RRBench tested locally executed open-weight agents on 20 longitudinal-data tasks creating 102 variables from six cohort sweeps. The largest tested models reached up to 87.9% average task completion.
- **Why it matters:** The open framework offers a measurable path for AI-assisted data preparation where governance rules prevent personal data from reaching cloud models.
- **Evidence limits:** The benchmark covers one cohort and evaluates preparation tasks, not downstream study estimates.

## What changed

Agentic AI is becoming part of the evidence stack itself. Read-only data substrates, executable provenance, curated retrieval, local models, and explicit expert gates are making large-scale automation more inspectable and more compatible with governed research environments.

## Ranked evidence

### 1. Agentic AI-enabled discovery across large-scale sleep physiology

- Direct source: [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25175), [experimental full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.25175v2)
- Publication date: July 29, 2026 (v2)
- Stable ID: `arxiv:2607.25175`
- Content version: `v2`
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. An expert-guided, multi-agent research environment performs hypothesis development, raw-signal feature engineering, cohort analysis, statistical modeling, and reporting over four clinical and epidemiological cohorts with linked longitudinal outcomes.
- What the primary source establishes:
  - AI Sleep Co-Scientist coordinates a Hypothesis Agent, Preprocessing Agent, and Execution Agent through a shared read-only data substrate and schema-validated Model Context Protocol layer.
  - The four cohorts contain approximately 124,000 polysomnography recordings and more than 50 TB of raw multichannel signals. The Stanford Sleep Clinic and Human Sleep Project include linked EHR diagnoses and longitudinal follow-up; BioSerenity supplies a multisite clinical network; the Central Disorders of Hypersomnolence registry supplies harmonized diagnoses.
  - Each reported number, table, and figure is linked to an executable script. Cohort definitions, canonical data splits, channel conventions, and analysis guidance are encoded in the shared substrate. Experts retain authority at hypothesis selection and before full-cohort commitments.
  - Five case studies addressed disease risk, physiological aging, comorbid insomnia and sleep apnea phenotyping, REM regulation, and narcolepsy microstructure.
  - In the Stanford discovery cohort, each 1-SD decrease in N2 brain-brain coupling was associated with subsequent Parkinson's disease (HR 1.48, 95% CI 1.31-1.67) and Alzheimer's disease (HR 1.38, 95% CI 1.25-1.53).
  - A physiologically structured late-fusion sleep-age model produced lower mean absolute error than early fusion in Stanford (7.06 vs 7.33 years) and in external Human Sleep Project validation without retraining (8.83 vs 9.52 years).
  - Across 2,496 disorder-feature pairs, effect sizes showed cross-site concordance of Pearson `r=0.631`. The control feature landscapes across 70 features were highly correlated (`r=0.979`).
- Why it matters: this is a concrete, cohort-scale example of agentic AI functioning as scientific infrastructure rather than a chat interface. The agents operate within a bounded data environment, produce reusable features, and leave executable provenance while domain experts steer the irreversible scientific choices.
- Evidence limits: the paper is a preprint. The analyses are retrospective and observational, so the disease associations are hypothesis-generating rather than causal. The cohorts reflect clinical referral and epidemiological sampling, single-night laboratory PSG, and uneven translation readiness. The safeguards reduce but do not eliminate long-horizon drift, hallucination, or model-version reproducibility challenges. None of the derived measures has been tested as a clinical decision tool.
- Follow-up: peer review; independent replication; prospective or target-trial-style validation of disease-risk hypotheses; multi-night and home data; public provenance artifacts; and measurement of researcher time, reproducibility across model versions, and the stability of findings across independent agent runs.

### 2. Intelligent Framework for Adverse Drug Event Identification Using Large Language Models and Retrieval-Augmented Generation

- Direct sources: [peer-reviewed article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13456140/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2196/84086), [open LLMADE repository](https://github.com/wuxuehong214/LLMADE)
- Publication date: August 10, 2026
- Stable ID: `doi:10.2196/84086`
- PMID: `42574561`
- PMCID: `PMC13456140`
- Content version: `version-of-record`
- Internal score: 10/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. Three LLMs with retrieval-augmented generation identify adverse drug events from deidentified, unstructured real-world clinical notes for pharmacovigilance and drug-safety research.
- What the primary source establishes:
  - The study began with 19,983 Chinese clinical notes from multiple tertiary hospitals, covering 2007-2023. After quality filtering and patient-level deduplication, 18,432 records from unique patients remained.
  - Clinical pharmacists reviewed machine-assisted preannotations. Agreement between two annotators on 5,535 notes was Cohen's `κ=0.86`.
  - Deduplication and balancing produced a 2,510-record gold-standard reference set and a 5,144-record ADE knowledge base. The authors publicly released the first Chinese clinical-note ADE research corpus through GitHub.
  - DeepSeek-V3 with RAG achieved L3 F1 of 0.9638 (95% CI 0.9541-0.9727) on the curated reference benchmark.
  - In the low-prevalence evaluation sampled from 19,983 uncurated progress notes, RAG achieved L3 recall 0.9448, F1 0.8159, specificity 0.9821, F2 0.8885, and Cohen's `κ=0.8057`. Nonaugmented generation produced L3 recall 0.8549 and F1 0.6380; static augmentation produced recall 0.9062 and F1 0.8134.
  - For GPT-4o on the curated benchmark, RAG increased L3 recall from 0.6419 without augmentation to 0.9241.
- Why it matters: the architecture makes pharmacovigilance knowledge updateable without repeatedly fine-tuning the core model. New ADE examples can enter the retrieval layer, while structured labels and retrieved cases make outputs easier to inspect. The open corpus supplies a reusable benchmark for Chinese clinical text.
- Evidence limits: the knowledge base and evaluation data came from the same overall source distribution, creating external-validity and possible leakage concerns. No head-to-head clinical BERT baseline was tested. The cloud API version of DeepSeek-V3 was used even though local deployment is discussed as a future privacy path. At the observed 4.6% ADE prevalence, the authors estimate that the 1.79% false-positive rate would produce about 17 false flags per 1,000 screened notes.
- Follow-up: multicenter external validation; prospective workflow evaluation; comparison with conventional clinical NLP; local deployment testing; threshold calibration; reviewer-workload measurement; and continued expansion of the ADE knowledge base.

### 3. Agentic coding without the cloud

- Direct sources: [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21482), [open RRBench repository](https://github.com/UCL-ARC/RRBench)
- Publication date: July 24, 2026 (v2)
- Stable ID: `arxiv:2607.21482`
- Content version: `v2`
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. Locally deployed open-weight LLM agents write and execute R code for longitudinal cohort-data preparation, including category harmonization and multi-wave merging, against a ground-truth benchmark derived from a British cohort study.
- What the primary sources establish:
  - RRBench contains 20 data-preparation tasks creating 102 variables from six sweeps of a British longitudinal cohort.
  - Tasks include recoding, category harmonization, composite-index construction, missingness handling, and multi-wave merging.
  - The agent runs locally, writes and executes R code in an isolated directory, and compares output tables with curated ground-truth data using the open-source `tabmatch` evaluator.
  - State-of-the-art open-weight models in the 31-35B parameter range achieved average task completion of up to 87.9% on consumer-grade hardware.
  - The repository includes task definitions, metadata, reference R scripts, agent code, experiment configuration, evaluation routines, tests, and an MIT license. Raw safeguarded cohort data must be obtained separately.
  - The work was presented at SLLS 2026 and accepted at CLS 2026 and RSS 2026; the arXiv paper remains a preprint.
- Why it matters: governed research environments often prohibit sending personal data to third-party model providers. RRBench demonstrates a practical path for bringing capable coding agents to the data, preserving local control while measuring outputs against an executable reference.
- Evidence limits: the benchmark covers 20 tasks from one cohort and evaluates data preparation rather than downstream study estimates. Near-saturation on this benchmark does not establish general performance across cohorts, statistical packages, languages, or novel analytical decisions. The current repository supports Ubuntu.
- Follow-up: cross-cohort benchmarks; blinded human comparison; downstream estimand checks; harder missing-data and linkage tasks; version-stable local deployment; and measurement of review time and correction burden.

## What changed

The new pattern is architectural. The strongest systems no longer ask readers to trust a single model response. They place agents behind read-only data access, local execution, structured retrieval, executable references, schema validation, and explicit expert checkpoints. That turns agentic AI from a conversational helper into a governed layer of the evidence stack.

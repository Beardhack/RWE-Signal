# The agents screened 5,598 papers. Their top first pass missed 43% of eligible studies.

**RWE-Signal - August 14, 2026 - Friday edition**

MacAma moved an entire meta-analysis workflow into a multi-agent system and published the code, benchmark materials, and human checkpoints. The claimed speed is remarkable; the first-screen recall is the reason the checkpoints matter.

## Agentic evidence synthesis reached the whole workflow

- **Source:** [Yuan et al., Smart Medicine](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13431750/)
- **Date:** July 29, 2026
- **What happened:** The peer-reviewed MacAma system connected literature retrieval, title/abstract screening, full-text screening, statistical analysis, and report writing in a protocol-constrained multi-agent workflow. Its radiotherapy case study began with 5,598 records.
- **Why it matters:** The authors estimate more than 18 months of conventional work was compressed into under four days, but the highest-F1 initial screen combined 94.25% precision with only 56.55% recall.
- **Evidence limits:** The speed figures are author estimates, the case study is preclinical, the two screening benchmarks are not directly comparable, and high-impact extraction, statistical, and interpretive steps still require expert verification.

## Friday synthesis

This week's systems got larger in scope—from cohort construction and safety review to regulation and end-to-end meta-analysis—but their control problem stayed familiar. Retrieval completeness, record observability, task-specific validation, and human adjudication still determine whether model output can become evidence.

## What changed

The new signal is not that agents can write a review. It is that a peer-reviewed, open workflow now exposes where agents can carry routine evidence work and where the error cost still requires a human gate. The next standard should measure missed studies and changed conclusions, not only elapsed time.

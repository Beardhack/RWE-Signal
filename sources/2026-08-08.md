# Research notes - 2026-08-08

## Editorial line

**Headline:** The AI never saw a patient row

A new agentic analytics design kept row-level records inside local R while a cloud model planned the analysis. Three additional studies show why that separation solves only one part of the evidence problem: external performance can fall, prognosis may require clinical notes that diagnosis does not, and scanned source documents can inject errors before the language model begins.

## Ranked evidence

### 1. Yang et al. - agentic statistical analysis with row-level data kept local

- Direct source: [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.30.26359367v1)
- Publication date: August 2, 2026
- Stable ID: `doi:10.64898/2026.07.30.26359367`
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. The source functionally validates an agentic, natural-language-to-R system by reproducing a published clinical prognostic-model analysis while keeping patient-level data in the local execution environment.
- What the source establishes:
  - The n8n-orchestrated system combined DeepSeek-Reasoner with Pinecone retrieval grounded in curated biostatistical guidance and R templates.
  - It supported schema perception, interactive cleaning, requirement refinement, and locally executed R code.
  - The external language model received no row-level patient data, according to the authors.
  - The validation reproduced a published prognostic-model study in metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease.
  - Natural-language dialogues produced data cleaning, multivariable Cox models, diagnostics, baseline tables, Schoenfeld residual checks, ROC curves, and forest plots without manual coding.
  - The authors report that the workflow reduced the analytic lifecycle from days to hours.
- Why it matters: the system separates two risks that are often bundled together. A cloud model can help specify and write an analysis without becoming the environment that reads the patient table. The local R artifacts also preserve an executable object for review.
- Limits: preprint; one reproduced prognostic study; no formal multicenter usability evaluation; no broad statistical-validity benchmark; the privacy claim addresses row-level data flow, not every possible disclosure channel or legal basis; local execution does not make generated methods correct.
- Follow-up: test method selection, estimands, missing-data decisions, code correctness, and result reproducibility across a preregistered battery of observational studies.

### 2. Zhang et al. - lupus disease activity lost accuracy outside the development setting

- Direct source: [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.09.26357586v1)
- Publication date: July 10, 2026
- Stable ID: `doi:10.64898/2026.07.09.26357586`
- Internal score: 8/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. The study derives the SLEDAI-2K longitudinal disease-activity phenotype from real EHR notes, externally validates it, and applies the resulting labels to long-term observational outcomes.
- What the source establishes:
  - Seven open-source LLMs were evaluated on a specialist-verified internal reference of 658 clinical notes and an external set of 56 MIMIC-IV discharge summaries.
  - A two-layer GPT-OSS-120B plus verifier achieved internal descriptor micro-F1 of 94.2% and exact SLEDAI-2K score agreement of 86%.
  - On the external set, micro-F1 was 87.7% and exact score agreement was 64%.
  - The workflow was applied to 2,576 serial notes from 108 patients.
  - LLM-defined sustained low disease activity was associated with lower incidence of stage 3 chronic kidney disease, kidney replacement therapy, and hospitalization over 18.3 years of follow-up.
- Why it matters: a modest decline in component classification became a 22-point decline in exact composite-score reconstruction. Downstream scores can amplify small descriptor errors.
- Limits: preprint; external validation used only 56 discharge summaries and differs from longitudinal specialist notes; 108-patient outcome analysis; retrospective associations are not causal effects; no prospective deployment.
- Follow-up: test site-specific score calibration, error concentration around clinically meaningful score thresholds, and cohort reclassification before using the phenotype in comparative studies.

### 3. Khan et al. - pathology could support diagnosis, but not complete prognosis

- Direct source: [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.06.26.26356633v1.full)
- Publication date: July 9, 2026
- Stable ID: `doi:10.64898/2026.06.26.26356633`
- Internal score: 8/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. The framework combines GPT-4 Turbo extraction with deterministic diagnostic and risk rules to construct research-ready phenotypes from longitudinal EHR records for three myeloproliferative neoplasms.
- What the source establishes:
  - The study used 60 patients for prompt development and a held-out test set of 450 patients, balanced across polycythemia vera, essential thrombocythemia, and myelofibrosis cases and controls.
  - Ground truth came from independent dual-clinician review with consensus adjudication.
  - The framework integrated pathology reports, notes, and structured labs, then applied WHO/ICC diagnostic criteria and NCCN-aligned risk models.
  - Diagnostic sensitivity was 100% for all three diseases; specificity was 98.7% for polycythemia vera and 100% for the other two diseases.
  - With all sources, reported risk-stratification accuracy was 100% across all three diseases.
  - Pathology-only diagnosis remained strong, but pathology-only prognostic accuracy fell to 69.3% for polycythemia vera, 93.3% for essential thrombocythemia, and 77.3% for myelofibrosis. Adding clinical notes restored reported prognostic accuracy to 100%.
- Why it matters: data fitness is endpoint-specific. A source sufficient for disease classification may omit prior events or contextual facts required for risk stratification.
- Limits: preprint; single institution; only 172 held-out patients had clinical notes; unusually high held-out performance needs prospective, multisite replication; balanced case-control testing does not reproduce real prevalence.
- Follow-up: report source availability by site and quantify how missing note types move risk groups and study eligibility.

### 4. Shrestha et al. - scanned sleep reports introduced errors before evidence extraction

- Direct source: [Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-58657-x)
- Publication date: July 12, 2026
- Stable IDs: `doi:10.1038/s41598-026-58657-x`; `pmid:42437760`
- Internal score: 7/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. The peer-reviewed study evaluates computer vision plus ChatGPT-3.5 for extracting variables from real image-based polysomnography reports explicitly to support evidence synthesis.
- What the source establishes:
  - Reports came from a tertiary-care sleep laboratory in Thailand.
  - A two-stage pipeline used DBNet inside EasyOCR to convert report images to text, followed by task-specific ChatGPT-3.5 prompts to extract feature values.
  - Prompt refinement reduced extraction error.
  - The recurring failures were numerical, symbol, and character-encoding errors across the document-conversion and LLM stages.
- Why it matters: an extraction metric cannot be interpreted without the upstream document pipeline. OCR errors can become apparently structured values before an LLM or researcher sees them.
- Limits: one sleep laboratory and document environment; the accessible publisher page currently exposes the abstract and early unedited manuscript notice but not detailed result tables; extensive testing across document qualities and sites remains necessary.
- Follow-up: preserve page-level provenance, measure OCR and LLM errors separately, and validate high-impact numerical fields after the full image-to-variable chain.

## What changed

The new evidence separates four layers that require different controls: remote model reasoning, local deterministic execution, transport of a phenotype to another record environment, and source-document conversion. A privacy boundary can keep rows away from the model, but it cannot validate statistical choices, make an endpoint portable, supply missing clinical context, or repair OCR errors.

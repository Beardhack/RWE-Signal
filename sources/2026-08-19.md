## Ranked evidence

### 1. PanoraOnc: A pan-cancer clinico-genomic AI model for transferable outcome predictions

- Direct source: [medRxiv v1](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.17.26354679v1) and [complete PDF](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.17.26354679v1.full.pdf)
- Posting date: August 18, 2026
- Stable ID: `doi:10.64898/2026.08.17.26354679`
- Content version: `v1`
- Authors: Manuel Schürch, Jacob Geisberg, Cameron T. Flower, Ayyuce Begum Bektas, Thomas O. McDonald, Satyakam Mishra, Christopher Graser, Jennifer Altreuter, Guruprasad Ananda, Genevieve Boland, David Liu, Kenneth L. Kehl, Franziska Michor, and colleagues
- Internal score: 10/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. An open-weight vision-language foundation model is pretrained and evaluated on multi-institutional real-world clinico-genomic cohorts, then used for transferable outcome prediction and observational counterfactual subgroup exploration.
- Novelty note: a June 2026 conference abstract previewed PanoraOnc. The August 18 v1 preprint is a material new artifact: it supplies the complete methods, cohort construction, non-overlap controls, outcome-specific transfer experiments, counterfactual analysis, limitations, and model-release terms used for this edition.
- What the primary source establishes:
  - The study covers 84,131 real-world cancer patients across 66 cancer types: 58,694 Dana-Farber Cancer Institute patients, 24,950 Memorial Sloan Kettering patients across five cancers, and 487 melanoma patients in the 8RNAWES cohort.
  - Inputs span demographic and clinical variables, treatment histories, targeted sequencing, whole-exome sequencing, RNA measurements, and imaging-derived immune features. Not every patient has every modality.
  - Patient records were encoded into JSON-like text and feature representations for a Gemma3-12B vision-language base model. PanoraOnc used low-rank adaptation with four-bit quantization.
  - The treatment decision point was defined as time zero, and only pretreatment features were supplied as model inputs. Outcomes included treatment response and one-, two-, and three-year survival.
  - Direct cohort-specific fine-tuning raised AUROC from 0.50-0.63 for the base model to 0.62-0.83. Pan-cancer pretraining then supported zero-shot and few-shot transfer without using cohort-specific training samples in the zero-shot tests.
  - In external zero-shot validation on the MSK pan-cancer, pan-treatment cohort, the DFCI-pretrained model reached AUROC 0.77, an improvement of 0.18 over the base model without MSK patient data in pretraining.
  - On DFCI NSCLC immunotherapy two-year survival, combined DFCI-and-MSK pretraining reached AUROC 0.69 and remained ahead of a model trained directly on the target cohort until about 1,020 local samples were available. The crossover point was lower in several other cohorts.
  - In the largest multimodal comparison, all-feature performance reached AUROC 0.82 versus 0.77 for clinical variables alone.
  - A 6,282-patient DFCI-and-MSK NSCLC analysis generated factual and hypothetical outcome predictions across chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and targeted therapy. The authors used propensity estimates and targeted maximum likelihood estimation to explore candidate treatment-benefit subgroups.
  - The interactive research agent, Dr. PanoraOnc, exposes quantitative and qualitative scenario exploration. MSK-based model weights are open; DFCI-based weights are restricted by consent and IRB requirements. Code is promised upon publication.
- Why it matters: pretraining on broad real-world clinico-genomic histories can create a reusable starting point for smaller local cohorts, reducing the amount of target data needed before an outcome model becomes useful. The separation of pretreatment inputs, held-out evaluation, non-overlapping pretraining and test cohorts, and external transfer tests make the claim unusually inspectable.
- Evidence limits: this is a preprint. Real-world endpoints differ across cohorts, representation across institutions and populations is incomplete, and gains narrow as local sample size grows. The counterfactual work depends on overlap and no unmeasured confounding and should be read as hypothesis generation rather than treatment recommendation. Attributions, candidate biomarkers, and the interactive agent need prospective clinical evaluation, and the DFCI-trained weights cannot be released.

### 2. Retrieval-Augmented Large Language Models for Clinically Aligned Adverse Event Coding in Acute Myeloid Leukemia Clinical Trials

- Direct source: [medRxiv v1](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.17.26360282v1) and [complete PDF](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.17.26360282v1.full.pdf)
- Posting date: August 18, 2026
- Stable ID: `doi:10.64898/2026.08.17.26360282`
- Content version: `v1`
- Authors: Naghme Dashti, Martin M. K. Schneider, Jan-Niklas Eckardt, Frank Fiebig, Dirk Schweigler, Sophia Büttner, Moritz Middeke, Martin Bornhäuser, Christoph Röllig, Jakob Nikolas Kather, and Isabella C. Wiest
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. A locally deployed retrieval-augmented LLM standardizes free-text safety observations from three prospective controlled AML trials into the MedDRA hierarchy and is evaluated against original coding plus independent clinical review.
- What the primary source establishes:
  - The retrospective secondary analysis used fully deidentified adverse-event descriptions from MOSAIC (`NCT04385290`), DELTA (`NCT02446145`), and DaunoDouble (`NCT02140242`). The datasets contributed 179, 226, and 443 unique descriptions respectively, for 848 total.
  - The system first retrieved the 100 most semantically similar MedDRA v25.0 Low Level Terms using `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` embeddings, with lexical fallback and random fill only when necessary. MedDRA contains more than 80,000 LLTs.
  - A locally run Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct model selected one machine-readable LLT from the candidates. Deterministic lookup then mapped that LLT to its Preferred Term and System Organ Class.
  - The coder-assigned LLT appeared in the dense-retrieval top 100 for 88% of MOSAIC descriptions, 76% of DELTA, and 80% of DaunoDouble, outperforming lexical retrieval in all three datasets.
  - Exact-match accuracy ranged from 50%-58% at LLT, 78%-85% at PT, and 90%-93% at SOC. A zero-shot LLM baseline was lower at each hierarchy level.
  - Two independent clinicians reviewed model selections for clinical acceptability, with a third resolving disagreements. Clinical Correctness Rate ranged from 91%-97%; 38%-41% of apparent LLT exact-match errors were judged clinically acceptable alternatives. Interreviewer Fleiss kappa ranged from 0.58 to 0.81.
  - Results were repeated over three random seeds. Five open instruction-tuned model backbones were compared; differences were largest at the LLT level while higher-level PT and SOC performance was more stable.
  - Code is promised upon publication. The underlying trial data remain private.
- Why it matters: the design puts a flexible model inside a controlled terminology workflow. Retrieval narrows the choice set, the model handles clinically variable phrasing, deterministic mapping preserves the MedDRA hierarchy, and clinician review makes the distinction between literal label agreement and clinically reasonable alternatives visible.
- Evidence limits: this is a preprint and a retrospective study in one disease area. The original coder LLT is itself subjective, while expert acceptability is not formal regulatory validation. The study does not yet measure prospective coding time, integration burden, user acceptance, or transport across therapeutic areas, institutions, languages, and MedDRA versions.

## What changed

Foundation models are becoming reusable layers of evidence infrastructure. PanoraOnc carries representations learned from broad, multimodal real-world oncology cohorts into new institutions and smaller target populations. The adverse-event pipeline carries free-text interpretation into a regulated terminology hierarchy with deterministic lineage and clinician adjudication. Both systems make transfer—not a one-off answer—the central capability.

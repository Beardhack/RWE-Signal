**Headline:** BALMS mapped the agent architecture for longitudinal mental-health sensing.

One previously unreported conference paper passed the hard intersection, novelty, evidence, and timeliness gates. ## Ranked evidence

### 1. BALMS: Benchmarking Agentic LLMs for Longitudinal Mental Health Sensing

- Direct source: [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27219) and the complete public TeX source.
- First public date: August 27, 2026 at 15:00:17 UTC; the arXiv record states acceptance to the EMNLP 2026 Main Conference.
- Stable ID: `arxiv:2608.27219`; story ID: `balms-agentic-longitudinal-mental-health-sensing-2026`; content version: `v1`.
- Internal score: 9/10.
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes as task-specific evaluation of LLM agents that predict and explain mental-wellbeing measures from de-identified longitudinal real-world phone and wearable records.** The agents operate directly on observational sensing histories and are evaluated for numerical and evidentiary grounding.
- What the source establishes:
  - BALMS uses three longitudinal datasets: DiversityOne, PMData, and GLOBEM. Their public releases contain 782, 16, and 497 participants, respectively.
  - The actual evaluation uses the DiversityOne Mongolia cohort (1,355 target-day/user samples), all 16 PMData participants (1,448 samples), and one continuous GLOBEM study term (1,640 samples).
  - Two tasks pair a closed-form wellbeing score with an evidence-grounded rationale. The benchmark compares four systems across three agentic paradigms—prompt based, memory based, and tool based—with five open and closed LLM backbones.
  - The best reported mean absolute error was 0.41 versus a 0.58 mean-predictor baseline on DiversityOne, 0.45 versus 0.48 on PMData, and 0.81 versus 0.80 on GLOBEM. These are dataset-native scales and should not be compared across cohorts.
  - Chain-of-thought improved reasoning-oriented backbones by as much as 41.4% in the tested configurations. Claude-Haiku-4.5 produced the strongest overall rationale quality in the paper's LLM-as-judge analysis.
  - With Qwen2.5-14B, longer retrieval history reduced RAG mean absolute error by about 29% on PMData and about 9% on GLOBEM. Prompt-only performance degraded as the look-back window grew, while the tool-based PHIA design was stable on PMData but brittle on raw mobile streams.
  - A Fitbit-only sensitivity analysis frequently matched or improved on the full mobile-plus-smartwatch input, showing that compact, semantically interpretable features can be more useful to current agents than additional raw streams.
- Limits: this is retrospective benchmark evaluation, not a prospective mental-health intervention or autonomous diagnosis. DiversityOne uses one country cohort; PMData contains 16 participants; GLOBEM uses one term and one anxiety subscale. Rationale quality is graded with an LLM-as-judge rather than clinicians or participants. The authors caution against generalizing the zero-shot results without local calibration and population-specific validation.
- Commercial context: one closed commercial backbone was included alongside four open-source backbones; the paper is an academic benchmark from Dartmouth College and the University of Cambridge.

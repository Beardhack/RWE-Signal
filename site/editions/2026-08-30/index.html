BALMS Mapped the Agent Architecture for Longitudinal Mental-Health Sensing

A new benchmark shows how selective memory, interpretable features, and schema-aware tools can turn long wearable histories into grounded evidence.

RWE-Signal  ·  August 30, 2026  ·  Sunday edition

The next generation of health agents will need to do more than answer questions about last week's step count. They will have to reason across months of sleep, activity, heart-rate, mobility, and phone-sensing data—then connect a prediction to the specific history that supports it.

BALMS, posted August 27 and accepted to the EMNLP 2026 Main Conference, turns that design problem into a common test. Four agent systems and five model backbones predict mental-wellbeing scores and generate evidence-grounded rationales across three de-identified longitudinal datasets. The result is a practical architecture map for patient-generated evidence: memory selection and data representation matter as much as the model itself.

Three longitudinal datasets gave the agents a real evidence horizon

BALMS joins phone and wearable histories with self-reported mood, stress, or anxiety outcomes. DiversityOne spans multiple mobile-sensing streams; PMData contributes Fitbit-style daily summaries; GLOBEM combines smartwatch and phone signals collected across repeated study terms. These are retrospective research cohorts rather than a live care deployment, but they preserve the central difficulty of real-world longitudinal evidence: long, irregular, multi-channel histories that cannot simply be pasted into a model prompt.

The benchmark compares three paradigms. A prompt-only system converts the history to text. Memory agents retrieve either the most relevant days or a hierarchy of time segments and summaries. A tool agent writes and executes code over in-memory data tables. Each approach is tested with Qwen, Mistral, DeepSeek, and Claude backbones, with and without explicit chain-of-thought prompting.

That shared setup is the advance. It evaluates an agent not only on whether it emits a plausible score, but on whether the supporting rationale names the right sensor, value, day, trend, and cross-stream relationship. Longitudinal health sensing becomes an evidence-grounding problem rather than a fluent-text demonstration.

Selective memory turned more history into better evidence

The strongest systems extracted useful signal without task-specific training, although no single architecture won everywhere. On DiversityOne, the best reported mean absolute error was 0.41, compared with 0.58 for a mean predictor that ignored the sensing inputs. On PMData, the best result was 0.45 versus 0.48. On GLOBEM, the simple baseline remained narrowly ahead at 0.80 versus the best agent's 0.81.

Longer history became valuable when the architecture could select it. With Qwen2.5-14B, expanding the retrieval window reduced the RAG agent's error by about 29% on PMData and about 9% on GLOBEM. The prompt-only system weakened as the window grew, while the code-writing agent stayed stable on PMData's daily aggregates but struggled to transfer the same operations to raw mobile streams.

The sensitivity analysis points in the same direction. A compact Fitbit-only view frequently matched or beat the full phone-plus-watch input. Current agents appear better able to reason over semantically meaningful features—sleep duration, resting heart rate, steps—than over a larger volume of raw measurements. The opportunity is not to discard multimodal evidence; it is to give agents an explicit representation layer that makes each signal computationally and clinically legible.

The new signal is an evidence-aware personal health agent

BALMS is a retrospective benchmark, not proof of an autonomous mental-health system. PMData contains 16 participants, the other datasets use restricted evaluation subsets, and rationale quality is scored with an LLM judge rather than clinicians or participants. Prospective testing, calibration, safety evaluation, and population-specific validation remain the path to responsible deployment.

What is new is a concrete engineering target. A capable longitudinal agent should retrieve the right slice of history, operate over a schema it understands, preserve the values behind its conclusion, and expose a rationale that can be checked against the record. That turns wearable streams from passive exhaust into reviewable real-world evidence—and gives the field a benchmark for building the next layer.

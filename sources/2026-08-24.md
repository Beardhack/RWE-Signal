# Research notes - 2026-08-24

## Editorial line

**Headline:** FDA put real-world monitoring at the center of generative-AI device oversight.

FDA's August 18 discussion paper proposes a lifecycle for generative-AI medical devices that pairs competency-based premarket evaluation with clinical confirmation using real or clinically representative data, then continues with re-benchmarking, clinician review of real-world inputs and outputs, drift monitoring, and possible machine-based supervisory agents. Newly released research artifacts show the same empirical-grounding pattern across trial-emulation code sets, causal discovery in pregnancy data, probabilistic planning from surgical notes, malaria-allocation decisions, and evidence-grounded ICD coding.

## Ranked evidence

### 1. Considerations for the Regulation of Generative AI-Enabled Medical Devices

- Direct sources: [FDA discussion page](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/digital-health-center-excellence/considerations-regulation-generative-ai-enabled-medical-devices-discussion-paper-and-request), [31-page discussion paper](https://www.fda.gov/media/194242/download), and [FDA announcement](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-seeks-public-feedback-inform-regulatory-approach-generative-ai-enabled-medical-devices)
- Publication date: August 18, 2026
- Stable ID: `fda:2026-genai-medical-devices-discussion-paper`
- Content version: `2026-08-18`
- Docket: `FDA-2026-N-7874`; comments due October 19, 2026
- Internal score: 10/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes as regulatory governance of GenAI and agentic systems using real-world clinical evidence**. FDA explicitly proposes clinical confirmation with RWD, ongoing review of real-world inputs and outputs, drift monitoring, and possible machine-based supervisory agents for GenAI-enabled devices.
- What the primary source establishes:
  - FDA is seeking input, not issuing draft or final guidance.
  - The possible risk framework has two axes: independence of device activity and severity of harm from relying on an incorrect output.
  - The possible competency-based premarket approach combines non-clinical benchmarking with clinical confirmation of the final user-facing device in real or clinically representative conditions.
  - FDA says confirmation approaches may incorporate RWD or outside-U.S. data and asks when synthetic data may supplement real patient evidence.
  - Possible postmarket approaches include periodic re-benchmarking, independent clinician review of sampled real-world interactions, and drift monitoring after changes in population, data environment, or model components.
  - FDA asks whether machine-based supervisory agents could facilitate postmarket monitoring and how the supervisory agents themselves should be evaluated.
  - The paper also addresses continuous changes and third-party foundation-model updates across the total product lifecycle.
- Why it matters: it makes real-world evidence part of the operating model for evaluating open-ended, evolving GenAI devices rather than a one-time adjunct to premarket testing.
- Evidence limits: this is a discussion paper and request for feedback, not policy. The approaches are framed as questions and possible structures; FDA says the paper does not state evidentiary expectations for future submissions or determine whether new legal authority would be needed.

### 2. TrialCode Agent: LLM-Assisted Clinical Code-Set Construction for Trial Emulation

- Direct source: [medRxiv DOI](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.20.26360962)
- Publication date: August 23, 2026
- DOI: `10.64898/2026.08.20.26360962`
- Content version: `v1`
- Authors: Amir Habibdoust, A. Sajjad, D. Hernandez, K. Patel, and X. Song
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes directly**. A hybrid LLM-terminology agent translates free-text trial criteria into computable code sets for reproducible real-world trial emulation across ICD-9-CM, ICD-10-CM, ICD-10-PCS, LOINC, and RxNorm.
- What the primary record establishes:
  - Evaluation used 40 criteria from 11 trial groups and expert-built reference code sets.
  - Best exact-code F1 was 0.680 for Claude with the baseline pipeline, 0.610 for GPT-5.5 with baseline, 0.548 for Qwen with hybrid biomedical RAG, and 0.383 for MedGemma with family expansion.
  - Hybrid RAG did not consistently improve aggregate F1, although it raised GPT-5.5 RxNorm F1 from 0.320 to 0.909.
  - Family expansion generally traded precision for recall.
  - The staged verifier raised micro-F1 from 0.254 before verification to 0.505 after final verification and expansion.
  - Vocabulary/existence checks removed 2,594 false-positive codes and acceptance filtering removed 952 more before controlled expansion.
- Why it matters: code-set construction is a consequential and auditable bridge between a protocol and the cohort queried from RWD. The agent uses deterministic terminology verification to make the output database-ready rather than treating the LLM's first interpretation as final.
- Evidence limits: this is a preprint abstract, and the full paper had not propagated to the public medRxiv page during the run. Forty criteria from 11 groups are a limited test set. The best-performing pipeline depended on the model, and deterministic validity checks cannot guarantee correct clinical interpretation.

### 3. Causal Modeling of Adverse Pregnancy Outcomes via Adaptive LLM Proposals

- Direct sources: [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21079) and [full HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21079)
- Publication date: August 21, 2026
- arXiv ID: `2608.21079`
- Content version: `v1`
- Authors: Kavimayil P. Komarasamy, Saurabh Mathur, Ameet Soni, David M. Haas, Kristian Kersting, and Sriraam Natarajan
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes directly**. CLARA uses LLMs as adaptive proposal distributions for causal graphs, scores each graph against observational pregnancy data, and feeds high-scoring structures back into subsequent generations.
- What the primary source establishes:
  - The real-data evaluation used 3,856 complete cases from the longitudinal nuMoM2b study, 9 early-pregnancy risk factors, and 4 adverse outcomes.
  - CLARA samples candidate DAGs, enforces expert-provided temporal constraints, scores structures with TreeBIC, and updates the in-context proposal distribution using top-scoring graphs.
  - With GPT-5.2 proposals on nuMoM2b, CLARA reported SID 0, recall 1.0, and precision 0.5 against an expert graph; one-shot GPT-5.2 reported SID 8.8, recall 1.0, and precision 0.5.
  - With Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct, CLARA reported SID 31.4 and recall 0.6, compared with one-shot SID 47.4 and recall 0.4.
  - The final graphs recovered all expert-listed edges in the GPT-5.2 setting and proposed additional clinically plausible relations.
- Why it matters: the LLM is used to explore hypotheses while observational evidence and constraints determine which hypotheses shape the next search step.
- Evidence limits: this is a preprint, and the expert graph is a clinical reference rather than known ground truth. Results were model-sensitive, precision remained 0.5 in the real-data comparisons, complete-case filtering reduced the cohort, and proposed edges require external validation before causal or intervention claims.

### 4. A Neurosymbolic Approach for Constructing Planning Domain Models from Clinical Narratives

- Direct sources: [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21186) and [full HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21186)
- Publication date: August 21, 2026
- arXiv ID: `2608.21186`
- Content version: `v1`
- Authors: Ranveer Singh, Saurabh Mathur, Michael Skinner, Prasad Tadepalli, Kristian Kersting, and Sriraam Natarajan
- Internal score: 8/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes as agentic scientific evidence infrastructure applied to real clinical narratives**. NSPIN converts surgical notes into structured sequences, probabilistic planning models, and empirically checked preconditions for decision support.
- What the primary source establishes:
  - Evaluation used 2,660 laparoscopic appendectomy notes written by 9 surgeons.
  - A local MedGemma-27B model handled extraction and imputation over sensitive narratives; Claude Sonnet 4.6 handled high-level schema creation and symbolic refinement.
  - LLM-proposed precondition revisions were accepted only when they did not worsen satisfaction or validation performance.
  - On held-out next-action prediction, NSPIN reported top-1 accuracy 0.42, top-3 accuracy 0.64, and true-action-disallowed rate 0.16, compared with 0.20, 0.27, and 0.68 for the LLM-only baseline.
  - Expert review found sampled imputations and effects clinically valid and generated procedures broadly coherent.
- Why it matters: it shows a concrete route from unstructured RWD to an executable world model whose rules can be inspected and tested rather than remaining inside a language model.
- Evidence limits: this is a preprint from one procedure type and one hospital. Expert qualitative review covered small samples, rare constraints remained underrepresented, and the induced model is decision-support research rather than a validated clinical deployment.

### 5. Institutionalizing LLM-assisted decision support for malaria risk-focused ITN reprioritization in Nigeria

- Direct sources: [medRxiv DOI](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.19.26360823) and [Malaria Reprioritization Tool project page](https://mesamalaria.org/resource-hub/malaria-reprioritization-tool/)
- Publication date: August 22, 2026
- DOI: `10.64898/2026.08.19.26360823`
- Content version: `v1`
- Authors: L. Mhlanga et al.
- Internal score: 8/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes as LLM-supported public-health evidence use**. ChatMRPT places a GPT-4o copilot over a malaria-risk planning engine that combines surveillance, socioeconomic, environmental, and transmission data for subnational insecticide-treated-net allocation.
- What the primary record establishes:
  - The mixed-methods implementation study involved 34 state malaria program officers from 28 Nigerian states.
  - Among 33 profiled participants, 19 had both higher digital competence and adequate workplace resources; the remainder had a limitation in one or both domains.
  - Officers prioritized contextual interpretation, operational decision support, and embedded analytical guidance.
  - Participants linked institutionalization to integration with routine planning, adaptability, digital competence, and basic infrastructure.
- Why it matters: the study moves the evaluation question from whether an LLM can summarize surveillance evidence to whether public-health decision makers can use it inside a recurring allocation workflow.
- Evidence limits: this is a preprint and formative implementation study, not an evaluation of resulting net allocations or health outcomes. Findings are based on a small, purposively relevant user group and reported experience rather than comparative effectiveness.

### 6. KREL: Automatic Medical Coding via Knowledge-Guided Reasoning over Clinical Evidence with LLMs

- Direct sources: [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20887) and [full HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20887)
- Publication date: August 21, 2026
- arXiv ID: `2608.20887`
- Content version: `v1`
- Authors: Xubin Chen, Yipeng Zhou, Wen Sun, Chengkai Huang, Xiaoming Fu, and Quan Z. Sheng
- Internal score: 8/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes as LLM-enabled structuring of longitudinal clinical records**. KREL grounds ICD assignment in full notes, localized evidence spans, structured code hierarchy, and coding rules, with evaluation on MIMIC-derived clinical records.
- What the primary source establishes:
  - Evaluation covered MDACE, ACI-BENCH, and MIMIC-IV, including a full ICD-10-CM space of 72,750 terminal diagnosis codes.
  - KREL reported F1 0.70 on ACI-BENCH and 0.49 on MDACE in the benchmark-label setting.
  - In full-label evaluation, it reported F1 0.51 on MDACE and 0.39 on a MIMIC-IV subset.
  - Removing the evidence verifier dropped precision from 0.49 to 0.10 while recall increased to 0.62.
  - Hierarchy-aware branch search improved retrieval recall to 0.70 versus 0.67 for flat dense retrieval and 0.46 for greedy hierarchical search.
- Why it matters: evidence-linked coding can make clinical-note variables more traceable for downstream cohorting, quality reporting, and research.
- Evidence limits: this is a preprint and benchmark study, not prospective deployment. Benchmark labels can be incomplete, evidence alignment was imperfect, commercial affiliations are present, and the authors call for human validation before clinical, administrative, or billing use.

## Ranked evidence

### 1. Clinical Laboratory Terminology Standardization for Semantic Interoperability Using a Large Language Model-Based Agent

- Direct source: [JMIR version of record](https://www.jmir.org/2026/1/e92499)
- Publication date: August 20, 2026
- DOI: `10.2196/92499`
- PMID: `42623484`
- Stable ID: `doi:10.2196/92499`
- Content version: `version-of-record`
- Authors: Lijuan Wu, Jinxin Huang, Hongnian Wang, Liyi Mai, Xueyun Zhan, Xinrong He, Xiaotang Zhang, Huiying Liang, Xin Li, and Abdelouahab Bellou
- Internal score: 10/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. An agentic LLM workflow standardizes two real-world emergency-department laboratory datasets to LOINC explicitly to support semantic interoperability and cross-institutional research.
- What the primary source establishes:
  - The retrospective study used deidentified real-world emergency-department laboratory data from Guangdong Provincial People's Hospital in Chinese and MIMIC-IV-ED in English.
  - LabBridge combines language-aware preprocessing, hybrid retrieval, constrained LLM reasoning, and final structural validation.
  - At full coverage across five tested LLMs, mapping accuracy ranged from 81%-90%.
  - With DeepSeek-V3, LabBridge reached 90% in the Chinese GDPH dataset versus 24% for BGE-M3 and 49% for the best RAG comparator. In the English MIMIC dataset it reached 90% versus 67% and 85% respectively.
  - GPT-4o reached 88%-89%; GPT-4o-mini reached 87%-90% across the two datasets.
  - Raw human interrater agreement was 63.4%; a senior physician adjudicated the remaining 36.6% of reference mappings.
- Why it matters: multilingual laboratory harmonization is a high-leverage prerequisite for cross-institutional analytics. The agent improves the full-coverage result while keeping retrieval, reasoning, validation, and human reference construction visible.
- Evidence limits: the study covers two languages and two institutions, uses a constrained LOINC Core subset, and scores correctness as a binary outcome. Final validation checks structure rather than performing semantic self-correction. Training-data contamination cannot be ruled out.

### 2. Lessons from preventable ICU-related coronial deaths: A large-language-model-assisted national analysis of adult cases in Australia (1997 to 2025)

- Direct source: [PubMed primary record and abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42623860/)
- Publication date: August 20, 2026
- DOI: `10.1016/j.jcrc.2026.155718`
- PMID: `42623860`
- Stable ID: `doi:10.1016/j.jcrc.2026.155718`
- Content version: `version-of-record`
- Authors: Henrique Mendes, Farzan Bolouki Yazdi, Chang-Yang Yew, and Ana Laura Gurgel
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. A versioned-prompt LLM pipeline directly structures and synthesizes 28 years of public real-world coronial health records for national ICU patient-safety evidence.
- What the accessible primary record establishes:
  - The study processed 8,583 public coroners' court reports using Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview in a two-stage, clinician-validated pipeline.
  - It identified 1,190 ICU-related cases and 563 cases meeting an explicit or potential preventability criterion. Median age was 52 years and 59.5% were male.
  - Leading presentations were trauma at 19.7% and perioperative or procedural care at 18.8%.
  - Frequent failure modes were communication, handover, or documentation at 63.6%; treatment, medication, or device issues at 50.6%; and diagnostic-process issues at 49.4%.
  - A fully crossed two-reviewer audit covered 130 cases. Cohen kappa was 0.64 for presentation and averaged 0.63 across failure modes.
- Why it matters: a national unstructured archive becomes usable for longitudinal safety learning through a reproducible pipeline with versioned prompts and clinician audit.
- Evidence limits: the full publisher article was not publicly accessible during this run. Evidence quality is therefore scored from the primary abstract and metadata rather than complete methods and tables.

### 3. Large Language Models for Oncology Guideline Maintenance: Prospective Case Study

- Direct source: [JMIR AI version of record](https://ai.jmir.org/2026/1/e93239)
- Publication date: August 20, 2026
- DOI: `10.2196/93239`
- PMID: `42623180`
- Stable ID: `doi:10.2196/93239`
- Content version: `version-of-record`
- Authors: Manuel Knauer, Julian Greß, Jakob Nikolas Kather, and Peter May
- Internal score: 10/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. Web-enabled frontier LLMs performed evidence retrieval, pivotal-trial data extraction, synthesis, prospective update prediction, and quality assurance inside a live oncology-guideline evidence lifecycle.
- What the primary source establishes:
  - In August 2025, Gemini 2.5 Pro and GPT o4-mini-high used deep-research and live-web capabilities to draft updates to a 2021 Onkopedia peripheral T-cell lymphoma guideline before the official October 2025 revision.
  - Against 30 substantive official changes, GPT reached a 40% update score and Gemini 36.7%. Blinded rescoring produced 38.3% and 35% with kappa 0.75.
  - The authors separately benchmarked evidence extraction across 80 pivotal trials and eight entities. Frontier models reached up to 99.2% accuracy on single-study extraction.
  - Performance varied across longer synthesis: GPT endpoint accuracy fell from 84.6% in the first half of the drafted guideline to 38.5% in the second half.
  - An audit of 28 updated guidelines found a median 16.5 formal errors per document, including clinically important invalid formulas and staging definitions.
  - Predictive drafts contained 62 errors for GPT and 36 for Gemini; GPT fabricated 12 studies.
- Why it matters: the study points toward a productive division of labor. Models already perform strongly on bounded trial-data extraction and systematic quality assurance, while expert authors retain synthesis, attribution, and final clinical responsibility.
- Evidence limits: the prospective update test covers one disease and national guideline. Model snapshots and proprietary retrieval limit reproducibility. Prompts were static, and the 28-guideline audit did not receive a complete human reaudit, so false-negative recall remains unknown.

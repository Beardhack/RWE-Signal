Agentic Validation Reached Across Oncology Submissions

A graph-constrained system added a cross-domain reasoning layer to oncology data review, as new agents widened evidence screening and clinical outcome capture.

RWE-Signal  ·  August 16, 2026  ·  Daily edition

The newest frontier in real-world evidence is verification at scale. Four studies published August 13 and 14 place AI inside concrete evidence workflows: checking oncology submissions, screening a six-figure literature search, finding ICU bleeding events, and ascertaining cardiovascular outcomes from longitudinal records.

The lead development is CAVE-Onc, a peer-reviewed system that gives validation rules and a constrained agent a shared graph of clinical-trial data. Its value is architectural: the system reads relationships that only appear when response, lesion, exposure, event, and timing records are considered together.

A graph gave the agent a view across domains

CAVE-Onc begins by translating CDISC submission datasets into an RDF knowledge graph. Its first layer contains 111 SHACL shapes: 85 ported from the CDISC CORE rule corpus, eight for RECIST 1.1 derivations, and 18 for cross-domain contradiction patterns. A LangGraph CaveAgent then handles the response logic that is easier to maintain as a structured decision workflow than as one monolithic query.

The agent queries target-lesion change, non-target response, and new-lesion status, applies the 34-row RECIST Table 7 matrix, and emits a trace when the derived and submitted overall responses disagree. Each step is typed and testable. The open MIT-licensed repository carries the benchmark, code, tests, committed evaluation outputs, mapping logic, and reproduction commands.

The cross-domain layer expanded what validation could see

In a preregistered construction test, CAVE-Onc detected all 20 injected contradiction archetypes. CORE detected eight and the Pinnacle 21 FDA engine detected six. The decisive class was the 10 cross-domain RECIST contradictions outside the CORE rule set: CAVE found all 10, while both domain-scoped comparison engines found none. The Layer 1-only ablation reached 19 of 20; the agent supplied the additional Table 7 detection.

The paper also probes transfer. Frozen shapes found three of five held-out archetypes. On two Project Data Sphere oncology trials mapped to SDTM, the engine stayed specific on unmutated data and detected 10 of 11 applicable injected contradictions in Synta and 16 of 18 in CA012. That is a meaningful bridge from demonstration data to real trial structures; evaluation on blinded raw submissions remains the next frontier.

ScreenAgent made a 201,064-record search reviewable

A new Johns Hopkins preprint applies a cascade agent to suicide-prevention evidence synthesis. ScreenAgent returns a structured include-or-exclude decision, sends candidate inclusions through a higher-effort second pass, and leaves final study inclusion with human reviewers.

The internal run found 43 of 44 eligible studies, reached 98.0% specificity, and reduced the human screening set by 99.4%. Processing the full corpus cost $855.91, or 0.43 cents per record. Agent-to-human-consensus agreement was also higher than the reported human-to-human comparison, with Cohen's kappa of 0.75 versus 0.64.

The two external results were cross-validated after prompt tuning rather than prospectively deployed, and the analytic code and data are available only on request. Independent, prospective use in other evidence domains would turn this strong feasibility result into a more general screening claim.

Clinical notes became a richer outcome layer

Two peer-reviewed studies show LLMs improving outcome ascertainment from narrative records. In Transfusion Clinique et Biologique, GPT-4o-mini screened 36,490 notes from 149 high-risk ICU patients. The adjudicated reference ultimately contained 654 true bleeding events; the LLM identified 647, including 588 not captured by the initial manual review. Eighty-five of its 739 candidates were not confirmed, and classification of bleeding site and severity reached 42% and 65%, respectively. The productive workflow is therefore high-recall discovery followed by expert adjudication.

A BMJ Open multisite validation tested an EMR-integrated zero-shot LLM against manually adjudicated cardiovascular outcomes in 3,684 patients: 2,258 treated with immune checkpoint inhibitors and 1,426 who underwent transcatheter aortic valve replacement. The LLM achieved the highest area under the curve in seven of eight outcome-and-cohort comparisons.

In the TAVR cohort, its AUCs were 0.915 for stroke, 0.928 for myocardial infarction, 0.844 for heart failure, and 0.862 for composite MACE. Improvements over ICD retrieval were statistically significant for stroke and MACE. In the cancer cohort, ICD retrieval remained slightly stronger for heart failure. The next opportunity is a hybrid system that selects narrative, code-based, or combined retrieval according to the outcome and cohort.

The new pattern is verified scale

Today's signal is not simply that models processed more data. Each advance wrapped AI in a verification structure: graph constraints and typed tools for submission checks, cascade review for literature screening, adjudication for bleeding events, and prespecified output rules against manually labeled cardiovascular outcomes.

That architecture makes scale useful. When every larger search, cohort, or submission also carries a traceable rule, a measurable miss rate, and a clear point of human authority, frontier AI can widen the evidence that researchers examine without making the result harder to inspect.

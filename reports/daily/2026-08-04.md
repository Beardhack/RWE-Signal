# RWE automation meets the protocol test

**RWE-Signal — August 4, 2026 — Daily edition**

A new open-source target-trial workflow makes advanced causal analyses easier to run, while FDA's FY2025 ledger and August research show why data fit and diagnostic discipline remain the hard part.

[Hisashi Noma's new TTE tutorial and R package](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01625) turn a long list of target-trial implementation decisions into a documented workflow: longitudinal data construction, treatment and censoring weights, diagnostics, survival models, standardization, competing risks, and bootstrap uncertainty. It is useful precisely because it does not pretend to choose the causal question. The examples are synthetic, the paper is a preprint, and the package documentation says users must still defend time zero, treatment strategies, outcomes, confounding control, and identification assumptions.

[FDA's current CDER accounting](https://www.fda.gov/science-research/real-world-evidence/real-world-evidence-submissions-center-drug-evaluation-and-research) shows the pipeline getting larger. Protocols containing RWD rose from 11 in FY2024 to 31 in FY2025, and NDA/BLA submissions containing RWE rose from one to ten. Four of those ten were approvals where RWE contributed to the decision; the rest included one approval where it did not, four complete responses, and one refusal to file.

Two August papers sharpen the data question. A [JAMIA Open study](https://academic.oup.com/jamiaopen/article/9/4/ooag124/8733482) used linked EHR and claims data to measure patient-level continuity, relate it to misclassification across 42 variables, and externally validate a prediction model. A [Dutch registry-pathology linkage study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42288045/) showed that changes in biomarker testing practice coincided with a large change in observed RET-fusion prevalence. Linkage expanded the field of view; neither study treated connection alone as proof that the resulting measure was fit for purpose.

Agentic RWE is also becoming a lifecycle claim. [EmulatRx](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-74501-2) coordinates five specialized agents across protocol retrieval, cohort construction, clinical review, and statistical analysis, but its evidence base still includes small task sets and synthetic causal tests. The [Journal of Biomedical Informatics special issue](https://www.sciencedirect.com/special-issue/334546/agentic-ai-for-real-world-evidence-life-cycle-support-scaling-trustworthy-evaluation-of-treatment-safety-and-effectiveness) now asks the field to evaluate full workflows, provenance, causal critique, governance, and safe deployment—not isolated tool calls.

The through-line is auditability. More analysis is becoming runnable; none of it is becoming self-validating.

# Research notes - 2026-08-09

## Editorial line

**Headline:** The agent ran the study. Humans rebuilt the cohort.

The strongest new signal is a governed agent that could orchestrate a 286,422-person hospital GWAS without receiving raw patient rows, yet still required human-directed repair of its hypertension definition. DoctorAgents and a three-site sequential fine-tuning study reinforce the same distinction: agents can automate execution and distributed adaptation, while cohort validity, transportability, and privacy remain separate empirical questions.

## Ranked evidence

### 1. Huang et al. - governed agentic GWAS exposed a phenotype failure

- Direct source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11084)
- Publication date: July 13, 2026
- Stable ID: `arxiv:2607.11084`
- Content version: `v1`
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. A governed agent system planned, executed, checked, and documented a genetic association study using hospital-linked genotype and EHR data, with explicit human repair of the real-world phenotype.
- What the source establishes:
  - The NVAITC AI Scientist used a governed execution environment to plan cohort extraction, orchestrate Kubeflow and PLINK2 workflows, generate quality-control artifacts, and draft research outputs.
  - The case study included 286,422 people in a hospital-linked genotype and EHR warehouse.
  - Raw protected health information stayed inside the authorized warehouse. The agent received aggregate row counts, summaries, plots, and manifests; external network access was blocked by default.
  - The expert phenotype identified 109,782 cases and 176,640 controls.
  - An early agent phenotype created 3,950 agent-case/expert-control disagreements. Of those people, 2,911 had blood pressure above 140/90 and 125 had an antihypertensive prescription.
  - Investigators performed a medication audit and directed reconciliation of the cohort logic.
  - After reconciliation, the workflow reproduced established associations at FGF5, ATP2B1, CNNM2, FTO, and GRB14. The study does not claim a novel locus.
  - Artifact inspection found a prior PLINK 0/1 label-encoding error that had silently excluded all samples.
- Why it matters: governance successfully constrained access and preserved auditability, but it did not answer the scientific question of who counted as a case. The most valuable agent behavior was visible execution and error recovery, not autonomous clinical judgment.
- Limits: preprint; one institutional environment; months of iterative capability engineering; replication rather than discovery; research and commercial team including NVIDIA's AI Technology Center; no evidence that phenotype reconciliation generalizes without expert intervention.
- Follow-up: evaluate the system on preregistered phenotype specifications, quantify human correction effort, and test whether disagreement review occurs before downstream results can influence the cohort definition.

### 2. Wang et al. - agent teams generated and repaired complete temporal-data pipelines

- Direct source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05375)
- Publication date: August 5, 2026
- Stable ID: `arxiv:2608.05375`
- Content version: `v1`
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. The study evaluates a multi-agent pipeline builder on three MIMIC-IV outcomes and longitudinal rheumatoid-arthritis treatment response, covering preprocessing, feature development, modeling, repair, evaluation, and optimization.
- What the source establishes:
  - DoctorAgents separates Consultant, Generator, Repairer, Logger and memory, Evaluator, and Optimizer roles. A domain-specific variant splits preprocessing from model development.
  - The tasks were MIMIC-IV mortality, seven-day readmission, length of stay longer than three days, and anti-TNF response from one to nine years of longitudinal Early Undifferentiated PolyArthritis data.
  - Five-fold stratified meta-train/meta-test evaluation used GPT-5 across agent frameworks.
  - DoctorAgents-DS reported AUPRC of 0.520 for mortality, 0.212 for readmission, and 0.704 for length of stay; rheumatoid-arthritis AUROC was 0.577.
  - Strongest reported comparator values were 0.509, 0.192, 0.692, and 0.564, respectively.
  - Removing shared memory reduced readmission validation AUPRC from 0.255 to 0.215; removing the Consultant reduced it to 0.246.
  - GPT-5.4 judged whether generated features were reasonable. The authors acknowledge that model-based judging is imperfect.
- Why it matters: the functional unit is becoming an iterative agent team rather than a single prompt. Shared memory and repair loops contributed to results, but the hardest longitudinal cohort remained only weakly predictable.
- Limits: preprint; small cohort; internal five-fold meta-test rather than external-site validation; modest absolute rheumatoid-arthritis performance; common GPT-5 backbone across agent comparisons; AI-as-judge feature review.
- Follow-up: repeat with clinician-blinded feature adjudication, locked external-site evaluation, cost and failure accounting, and prospective assessment of agent-generated preprocessing choices.

### 3. Liu et al. - sequential adapters approached pooled training across three sites

- Direct source: [JAMIA Open](https://academic.oup.com/jamiaopen/article/9/4/ooag139/8738251)
- Publication date: July 20, 2026
- Stable IDs: `doi:10.1093/jamiaopen/ooag139`; `pmid:42519595`
- Content version: `version-of-record`
- Internal score: 8/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. The peer-reviewed study uses real notes from three institutions to test a distributed LLM adaptation method for extracting a research-ready mobility phenotype without pooling raw text.
- What the source establishes:
  - The corpus contained 600 clinical notes, 200 from each of three institutions, and 3,810 labeled sections. The institutions included an academic referral center and two community practices in the upper Midwest.
  - Llama 3.1 8B was fine-tuned with low-rank adapters sequentially across sites. Raw notes remained at each institution; adapted weights moved onward.
  - Sequential versus centralized joint training achieved F1 of 0.861 versus 0.894 for mobility extraction and 0.912 versus 0.900 for impairment classification.
  - Patient-level sequential F1 was 0.914 for extraction and 0.943 for impairment classification.
  - A jointly fine-tuned 8B model achieved 0.894 and 0.900, compared with 0.661 and 0.796 for an untuned 70B model.
  - Errors included confusion around falls and wheelchairs, difficulty interpreting FOTO and TUG scores, and treatment goals mistaken for current status.
  - The authors explicitly warn that transferred parameters may leak information through inversion or membership-inference attacks and call for formal privacy audits, secure aggregation, or differential privacy.
- Why it matters: small, task-specific updates can preserve local custody of raw clinical text and perform near pooled training, but data locality cannot be equated with a proven privacy guarantee.
- Limits: three sites in one region; fixed site order; modest data volume; no formal privacy audit; raw notes unavailable; untested resistance to parameter attacks.
- Follow-up: rotate site order, add geographically independent institutions, run membership-inference and inversion audits, and quantify performance under differential privacy.

## What changed

The evidence now supports a sharper division of labor. Agents can operate inside explicit technical boundaries, generate and repair analysis pipelines, and move learned parameters between data custodians. Scientific authority does not follow automatically from that capability. Phenotype definitions, external validity, and privacy of derived artifacts still require separate human-led tests.

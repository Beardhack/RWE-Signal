**Headline:** LLMs turn clinical notes into future queries and structured evidence.

Three August 24 primary artifacts form one technical through-line. Future Querying converts longitudinal ICU notes into time-indexed questions with reference answers from later events. ## Ranked evidence

### 1. Future Querying: Can LLMs Serve as Implicit Medical World Models?

- Direct source: [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23248) and [complete experimental HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23248)
- Publication date: August 24, 2026; v1
- Authors: Siri Willems, James Butterworth, Lore Goetschalckx, Peter Vrancx, Philippe Modard, Elke Giets, and Ludovic Denoyer
- Stable ID: `arxiv:2608.23248`
- Story ID: `future-querying-mimic-icu-world-model-2026`
- Content version: `v1`
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes as an LLM-based evidence-dataset and outcome-query workflow over longitudinal RWD**. LLMs create time-indexed clinical questions from raw observational timelines and answer them against held-out future events in 9,733 MIMIC-IV ICU patient records.
- What the source establishes:
  - The MIMIC benchmark includes 9,733 patients, 164,911 queries, and a patient-wise 80/10/10 split.
  - An LLM extracts a predicate from a later report, selects a virtual present, formulates a future query, and rejects examples whose answer is already obvious from the observed history.
  - LoRA fine-tuning improved three small open models by 11.7-21.5 percentage points on MIMIC, bringing them to 43%-46% accuracy.
  - Among off-the-shelf models, only Claude Sonnet 5 and GLM-5.2 narrowly exceeded 50% on the real-note benchmark.
  - The paper is accepted to the first MICCAI Workshop on Medical World Models.
- Why it matters: the framework creates a reusable, endpoint-agnostic evidence layer over unstructured longitudinal records and shows that local parameter-efficient training can close much of the gap to larger proprietary systems.
- Evidence limits: arXiv v1; Gemini 2.5 Flash is used as the answer judge; only a subset was manually checked; no clinician upper bound, uncertainty model, prospective validation, or proof that performance reflects causal world modeling rather than pattern recognition.

### 2. Developing an open-source framework for LLM evaluation of patients using EHR clinical documentation

- Direct source: [medRxiv v1](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.21.26361031v1) and [DOI](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.21.26361031)
- Publication date: August 24, 2026; v1
- Authors: Liam Barrett et al.; corresponding institution University College London
- Stable ID: `doi:10.64898/2026.08.21.26361031`
- Story ID: `ent-ehr-llm-human-extraction-benchmark-2026`
- Content version: `v1`
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes as an LLM evaluation framework for structuring real-world clinical documentation**. Seven LLMs extract seven SNOMED-coded evidence categories from public ENT EHR notes and are compared with 14 medical professionals.
- What the source establishes:
  - The study uses 98 public ENT documents, 14 medical professionals, seven LLMs, and seven extraction categories.
  - Clinician-to-clinician Cohen's kappa was 0.752 (95% CI 0.710-0.794); LLM-to-clinician kappa was 0.391 (95% CI 0.362-0.420).
  - Bayesian posterior mean agreement was 0.813 among clinicians and 0.659 between clinicians and LLMs, a difference of 0.154; non-inferiority was rejected at margins 0.05, 0.10, and 0.15.
  - GPT-4o achieved 97.0% precision, 84.9% recall, and a 7.5% false-positive rate.
- Why it matters: the work benchmarks extraction against human variability and supports a bounded first-pass role that can accelerate conversion of narrative notes into standardized evidence fields.
- Evidence limits: medRxiv preprint; small single-specialty public corpus; annotators were study authors; no prospective workflow, multisite generalization, or downstream RWE analysis was evaluated.

### 3. Evaluating Clinical Concept Extraction and Evidence-Bounded Terminology Linking

- Direct source: [medRxiv v1](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.20.26360740v1) and [DOI](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.20.26360740)
- Publication date: August 24, 2026; v1
- Authors: Yibo Chen and Mihail Popescu; Washington University in St. Louis
- Stable ID: `doi:10.64898/2026.08.20.26360740`
- Story ID: `multisite-concept-extraction-terminology-linking-2026`
- Content version: `v1`
- Internal score: 8/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes as a foundation-model and LLM pipeline for extracting and linking clinical concepts from multisite RWD**. The source directly evaluates MedGemma, Gemma, and Llama pipelines on de-identified clinical notes from three health systems and tests how retrieved terminology evidence changes downstream link decisions.
- What the source establishes:
  - Study 1 compares five automated pipelines with two annotation sets in 66 notes from three health systems.
  - Human inter-annotator embedding-match F1 was 0.75. Automated embedding-match F1 was 0.55 for MedGemma, 0.53 for Gemma, 0.43 for sci_md and SciBERT, and 0.32 for Llama 3.3.
  - Complete retrieval returned a reference-matched link for 28/28 known-concept spans. With matched concepts masked, the LLM-only condition assigned 25/28 to possible candidates and declared 3/28 linked.
  - Full retrieval classified all 84 previously unmatched terms as plausible existing concepts and proposed none for extension; extension status was not independently adjudicated.
- Why it matters: separating extraction, retrieval, link decisions, and extension triage creates an auditable data-engineering pattern for AI-enabled evidence pipelines.
- Evidence limits: medRxiv preprint and pilot-scale component studies with different units of analysis; no end-to-end outcome measure or independently adjudicated ontology-extension reference.

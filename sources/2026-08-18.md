## Ranked evidence

### 1. A Locally Executed Agentic Artificial Intelligence Framework for Spine Surgery Systematic Reviews

- Direct sources: [open peer-reviewed article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13472668/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2106/JBJS.OA.26.00102), and four public MIT-licensed repositories for [deduplication](https://github.com/ORG-Karur-DataCenter/Systematic_review_DeDuplication_agent), [screening](https://github.com/ORG-Karur-DataCenter/Systematic_review_screening_agent), [extraction](https://github.com/ORG-Karur-DataCenter/Systematic_review_extraction_agent), and [verification](https://github.com/ORG-Karur-DataCenter/Sys_review_extraction_validation_agent)
- Electronic publication date: August 18, 2026
- Stable IDs: `doi:10.2106/JBJS.OA.26.00102`; `pmid:42598346`; `pmcid:PMC13472668`
- Content version: version of record
- Authors: Sathish Muthu, Dhibin Vikash Kolarpatti Ponnusamy, Vibhu Krishnan Viswanathan, Sathish Kumar Rajappan Chandra, and Khan Sharun
- Internal score: 10/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. Four coordinated agents execute deduplication, screening, extraction, and verification tasks in de novo systematic reviews, a direct evidence-synthesis workflow, with independent human review and open implementation artifacts.
- What the primary source establishes:
  - The framework was developed on 26 prior reviews and independently validated on three new spine-surgery reviews covering GLP-1 analogs, cervical giant-cell tumors, and return to work after spinal cord injury.
  - Independent reviewers established human ground truth through blinded dual review and consensus. The three reviews contained 6,214 records and ultimately included 72 studies.
  - A deduplication agent used DOI, title, author, and year matching and retained uncertain records. A two-pass title-and-abstract agent finalized only matching decisions. Full-text eligibility remained human-only. A two-pass extraction agent populated predefined fields, while a verification agent preserved logs and routed discrepancies for adjudication.
  - Agreement with human review was κ=0.98 for deduplication, κ=0.91 for title-and-abstract screening, and κ=0.87 for extraction. The agent sent 399 records to full-text review versus 329 from humans, deliberately favoring sensitivity; all 70 extra records were excluded by human reviewers.
  - The reported evaluated-task time was 5.8 agent-hours versus 65.5 human-hours, a 91.1% reduction. Extraction produced 1,446 concordant fields out of 1,550. Verification flagged 104 discrepancies, including 15 critical errors, all of which humans corrected.
  - The architecture was run through researcher-controlled tools on a 16-GB workstation. The screening and extraction components used Gemini/Antigravity services; “locally executed” describes workflow control and orchestration, not offline local-model inference.
- Why it matters: the study offers a modular, inspectable blueprint for agent-assisted evidence synthesis and evaluates each handoff against independent human work rather than reporting only an end-to-end speed claim.
- Evidence limits: this is one research group and three spine-surgery reviews. Full-text screening remained manual, and the workflow depends on proprietary, versioned model services and technical setup. The abstract and main results differ slightly on mean hours saved and screening reduction; the table and results support 19.9 hours saved per review and 91.1% total evaluated-task time reduction. External specialty-level validation remains necessary.

### 2. REFINE: Closing the Loop Between Large Language Models and Symbolic Rules in Clinical NLP

- Direct source: [medRxiv v1](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.11.26360118v1)
- Posting date: August 17, 2026
- Stable ID: `doi:10.64898/2026.08.11.26360118`
- Content version: `v1`
- Authors: Nan Wang, Alexandra Kakadiaris, Chenyu Li, Rongrong Wang, Jaerong Ahn, Yashan Wang, and Sunyang Fu
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. LLMs analyze multi-site EHR extraction errors and generate candidate symbolic rules that are re-run on a held-out error set, directly improving an RWD phenotyping and concept-extraction layer.
- What the primary source establishes:
  - The source data were error reports from a prior four-institution evaluation of NLP-CAM for cognitive and neuropsychiatric concepts in EHR notes at UT Physicians, Memorial Hermann, Harris County Psychiatric Center, and Beth Israel Deaconess.
  - Cleaning produced 754 false-positive and false-negative instances, split 50/50 by clinical concept into training and held-out test sets.
  - GPT-5.2, GPT-4o, and GPT-4o-mini, accessed through a HIPAA-compliant Azure OpenAI environment, classified error types, generated reasoning, and proposed regular-expression rules under four prompt conditions.
  - The generated rule sets replaced the manually curated baseline rules for the experiment. The baseline F1 was 0.37; GPT-5.2 with reasoning reached 0.58, GPT-4o reached 0.50, and GPT-4o-mini reached 0.45.
  - Reasoning-rich prompts generally performed best. The system preserved a symbolic output layer: candidate rules are explicit and can be inspected, edited, and deployed through the open-source localhost REFINE interface.
- Why it matters: REFINE turns LLM reasoning into portable, auditable rule candidates for heterogeneous EHR text rather than requiring a large model to process every record at production scale.
- Evidence limits: this is a preprint. It evaluates 754 error reports rather than the complete note corpus, the dataset is small and imbalanced across sites and concepts, generated rules were not clinically validated by domain experts, and the experimental rules replaced rather than augmented the original curated rules.

### 3. Large language models as synthetic clinical experts to inform longitudinal rare-disease modeling

- Direct source: [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16507) and [complete PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.16507)
- Submission date: August 17, 2026
- Stable ID: `arxiv:2608.16507`
- Content version: `v1`
- Authors: Clemens Schächter, Astrid Pechmann, Janbernd Kirschner, Jan Hasenauer, and Harald Binder
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. Locally executed LLMs generate clinical judgments that directly supervise representation learning on longitudinal SMArtCARE registry data and are tested against future motor milestones.
- What the primary source establishes:
  - The RWD comprised exactly 13,000 HINE-2 motor-function observations from 994 children with spinal muscular atrophy, with a median 12 visits per patient and 3.5 years of follow-up.
  - Local Gemma 4, GPT-OSS, and Qwen 3 models answered pairwise SMA-type questions from textual motor-profile descriptions. Consistency checks tested contradictions, label-order effects, and uncertainty before outputs were retained.
  - A differentiable surrogate learned the retained LLM judgments. Its output distribution was then added to a conditional variational autoencoder loss, discouraging reconstructions that were numerically close but crossed a clinically meaningful SMA-type boundary.
  - Depending on model and supervision weight, original-versus-reconstructed label disagreement fell from roughly 9%-11% to 6%-7% while moderate weights preserved numerical reconstruction quality.
  - In patient-grouped five-fold cross-validation, supervised latent representations generally improved one-year sitting, standing, and walking milestone prediction over the unsupervised latent representation and HINE-2 baselines. For example, GPT-OSS-120B supervision at weight 25 reduced sitting Brier score from 0.097 to 0.083 and increased AUC from 0.944 to 0.954.
  - The anonymized registry data and all LLM inference remained inside secure local computing environments. Code is promised upon publication; registry data cannot be public for privacy reasons.
- Why it matters: this is a new interface between language-based clinical knowledge and statistical modeling. The LLM is queried offline, audited, and distilled into a training signal, allowing the final longitudinal model to operate without repeated LLM calls.
- Evidence limits: this is a preprint and a single rare-disease registry. Synthetic-expert labels are prompt-, definition-, and model-dependent; consistency checks do not prove clinical correctness. The learned surrogate adds approximation error, supervision weight requires tuning, and stronger clinical validation is still needed.

## What changed

The new signal is an increasingly explicit control architecture for AI-enabled evidence. Agents can now divide review work into verified stages; LLM reasoning can be compiled into inspectable rules; and synthetic clinical judgments can be distilled into statistical objectives. The common pattern is capability with durable handoffs: every model output becomes a record, rule, or loss term that the rest of the evidence system can test.

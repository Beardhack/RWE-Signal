# FDA set a path from GenAI discussion to formal device guidance

**RWE-Signal - August 25, 2026 - Daily edition**

FDA's digital-health director says the agency expects to turn its new generative-AI device discussion into policy guidance. The evidence architecture under consideration links competency testing to real or clinically representative confirmation and continuing postmarket observation.

## A discussion paper gained a policy destination

- **Sources:** [STAT interview report](https://www.statnews.com/2026/08/24/fda-rick-abramson-generative-ai-guidances-are-coming/) and [FDA discussion paper](https://www.fda.gov/media/194242/download)
- **Date:** August 24, 2026
- **What changed:** Rick Abramson, director of FDA's Digital Health Center of Excellence, told STAT that FDA expects to issue formal policy guidance for generative-AI medical devices.
- **Why it matters:** Last week's exploratory framework can now be read as an early map of the evidence questions likely to shape formal policy, including testing on real or clinically representative inputs and monitoring after deployment.
- **Evidence limits:** No draft guidance or timetable is public. The interview report is subscriber-only, and the August 18 FDA document remains a request for feedback rather than current policy.

## The evidence lifecycle remains the substantive proposal

FDA is considering a progression from non-clinical competency benchmarks to clinical confirmation and risk-proportionate postmarket monitoring. Possible evidence sources include RWD, retrospective records, prospective studies, sampled real-world interactions, re-benchmarking, and drift detection. The opportunity is a policy structure that evaluates evolving GenAI devices as continuously evidenced systems rather than one-time static models.

## The signal

The genuinely new signal is FDA's stated destination: formal guidance. The detailed standards are still open, but developers and evidence teams now have a clearer reason to prepare traceable benchmark definitions, clinically representative validation, and postmarket evidence plans.

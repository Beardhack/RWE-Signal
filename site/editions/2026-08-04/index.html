Two Architectures Put Guardrails Around AI for RWE

EmulatRx distributes trial emulation across five agents; THESEUS confines the LLM to a reviewable step before deterministic code generation

August 4, 2026 — Daily edition

Two July papers offer unusually concrete answers to the same question: where should an AI model be allowed to act inside a real-world evidence workflow? One divides the lifecycle among specialists. The other makes the model stop at a structured specification.

EmulatRx coordinates trial, informatics, clinical, and statistical agents across 20 trial emulations. THESEUS translates target-trial prose into JSON, lets humans review it, and gives code generation to deterministic rules.

Neither establishes autonomous, decision-grade RWE—and both are more credible for showing exactly where automation ends.

EmulatRx turns trial emulation into a five-agent handoff

EmulatRx, published in Nature Communications on July 7, is not a general chatbot wrapped around an electronic health record. It assigns protocol retrieval, cohort construction, clinical interpretation, causal analysis, and synthesis to specialized agents under a supervisor.

The system was tested on 20 clinical trials: ten acute-care studies using MIMIC-IV and ten chronic-disease studies using INSIGHT. The workflow stores intermediate state and transformations, caches model responses, and fixes random seeds for downstream tools. Those choices do not guarantee reproducibility, but they make the chain of evidence more inspectable than an unstructured conversation with a model.

The most telling applied example came from unstructured notes: the system identified 33 additional eligible patients in one emulation who were not discoverable from structured EHR fields alone. That is a practical RWE contribution. It also creates a review obligation, because expanded ascertainment changes the study population and therefore the evidence.

The strongest EmulatRx result remains task-level

On 266 manually annotated eligibility concepts, GPT-4o reached 98.9% recall and 96.7% precision. Those are strong results for turning protocol language into computable concepts. They are not end-to-end validation of the causal conclusions produced by the full system.

Some causal evaluations used synthetic cohorts, the clinical review involved three experts, and the trial set was small. The evidence supports a multi-agent architecture performing selected RWE tasks; it does not show that the combined workflow can independently produce regulatory-grade evidence.

THESEUS gives the LLM one bounded job

THESEUS, published in JAMIA Open on July 9, uses a different architecture. The model interprets a natural-language target-trial description and maps it into structured JSON. A human can accept or reject field-level changes. Deterministic rules then generate Strategus R code for an OMOP environment.

The separation is the important result. A fluent generated script can hide a changed estimand or unsupported analytic choice. A structured intermediate artifact gives a reviewer something precise to inspect before executable analysis code exists.

Thirty studies reveal a familiarity advantage

The researchers tested eight language models on 30 study descriptions: 15 from the OHDSI ecosystem and 15 from outside it. Primary-analysis specification accuracy ranged from 0.93 to 0.97 for OHDSI studies and from 0.82 to 0.95 for non-OHDSI studies. The wider, lower range outside OHDSI suggests that reporting conventions and ecosystem familiarity still matter.

THESEUS covers four core specification groups: study period, time at risk, propensity-score adjustment, and outcome modeling. Cohort definition remains outside scope, unsupported analytic choices are excluded, and the study did not test time saved in live analyst workflows. The system is therefore a bounded protocol-to-code aid, not an automated target-trial study.

What changed: architecture became part of the evidence

The new signal is not that an LLM can produce RWE on demand. It is that two research teams made the model's role explicit enough to evaluate. EmulatRx distributes responsibility across specialized agents and records the handoffs. THESEUS confines model judgment to protocol interpretation, inserts human review, and makes code generation deterministic.

The practical question is shifting from Can the model do the task? to Can every consequential choice be inspected, challenged, and reproduced? That is a more demanding test—and a more useful one for real-world evidence.

## Evidence reviewed

### Context only: Dai et al. — exposure definitions reverse a target-trial result

- Direct source: [PMC full text of the iScience paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13355737/)
- Date: July 7, 2026
- Identifiers: DOI 10.1016/j.isci.2026.116584; PMID 42436975
- Internal score: 9/10
- Establishes: in MIMIC-IV, two pre-specified exposure definitions for balanced crystalloids versus saline produced opposite MAKE-30 estimates in the same 42,883-patient 48-hour cohort. Initial assignment produced OR 0.49 (95% CI 0.46–0.55); 48-hour dominant fluid produced OR 2.51 (2.31–2.72). Both diverged from the SMART trial benchmark of about 0.90.
- Diagnostics: overlap weighting reduced the maximum standardized mean difference across 23 baseline covariates to 0.048. Yet 89.4% of patients switched between first fluid and 48-hour dominant fluid. Negative controls moved in the same opposite directions as the primary outcome.
- External comparison: recorded switching in eICU-CRD was 1.2%, and the operationalization discrepancy was absent, although the authors caution that infusion capture varies across eICU sites.
- Why it matters: a clean balance table did not detect time-varying institutional workflow embedded in the exposure definition. The study argues for operationalization sensitivity, switching characterization, negative controls, DAG review, and cross-database comparison.
- Limits: this is a diagnostic methods case study, not new clinical evidence about fluids. MIMIC-IV is single-center, the negative controls depend on length of stay, time-varying causal methods were not tested, and eICU infusion completeness is variable.

### Context only: Fan et al. — advanced adjustment does not cure confounding by indication

- Direct source: [Nature Communications article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-74999-6)
- Date: July 13, 2026
- Identifiers: DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-74999-6; PMID 42443168
- Internal score: 9/10
- Establishes: using CPRD Aurum, the authors emulated heart-failure trials for beta-blockers as a positive control and digoxin as a negative control. They compared propensity-score matching, inverse probability weighting, targeted maximum likelihood estimation, and a Transformer-based approach.
- Result: no method reproduced the randomized benchmarks. All suggested neutral or harmful beta-blocker effects and increased mortality for digoxin. In semi-synthetic simulations where confounders were observed, the same methods recovered the true effects.
- Why it matters: the failure is attributed to residual confounding in the real-world comparison rather than lack of estimator sophistication. A more flexible model cannot adjust for decisive information the data do not contain.
- Limits: this is a heart-failure test case in one UK data source. Controls and simulations make it an unusually useful diagnostic study, but they do not establish that all TTEs or advanced adjustment methods fail.

### Context only: Chou et al. — agentic causal analysis works better as constrained scaffolding

- Direct sources: [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22443), [public oci-agent repository](https://github.com/Netflix-Skunkworks/oci-agent)
- Date: July 24, 2026
- Identifier: arXiv 2607.22443
- Internal score: 9/10
- Establishes: oci-agent divides responsibility among a human principal, an actor that runs analysis, and a critic that reviews diagnostics. It uses deterministic, re-executable notebooks built on established libraries rather than asking an LLM to invent the statistical routine.
- Evaluation: across 231 ACIC 2016 datasets, the critic classified 192 estimates as satisfactory and 39 as unsatisfactory; the satisfactory group had lower RMSE and better confidence-interval calibration. On ten selected ACIC datasets, prompt-only output was uncorrelated with ground truth while the full scaffold recovered ground truth in nine. In disguised LaLonde tests, the full system reached median absolute percentage error of 52% on PSID and 19% on CPS, and withheld PSID certification in 19 of 20 repetitions because overlap was poor.
- Deployment claim: the authors report more than 100 internal Netflix analyses per month since June.
- Why it matters: the agent's value comes from enforced diagnostics, inspectable artifacts, estimand changes, and the ability to refuse certification—not fluent statistical prose.
- Limits: the paper is a preprint authored by the builder, its applied cases are commercial rather than clinical, and the public benchmarks do not validate the system on healthcare RWD.

### 4. Kim et al. — THESEUS narrows protocol-to-code automation to a schema

- `ai_rwe_intersection`: passes. The primary source materially evaluates LLM-based specification extraction inside target-trial emulation and deterministic RWE code generation.

- Direct source: [JAMIA Open article](https://academic.oup.com/jamiaopen/article/9/4/ooag131/8729191)
- Date: July 9, 2026
- Identifiers: DOI 10.1093/jamiaopen/ooag131; PMID 42428529
- Internal score: 8/10
- Establishes: THESEUS uses an LLM to map natural-language TTE descriptions into a reviewable JSON specification, then uses deterministic rules to produce Strategus R scripts in the OHDSI ecosystem.
- Evaluation: eight LLMs were tested on 15 OHDSI and 15 non-OHDSI studies. Primary-analysis standardization accuracy ranged from 0.93 to 0.97 for OHDSI studies and 0.82 to 0.95 for non-OHDSI studies.
- Why it matters: the system turns open-ended code generation into constrained extraction plus deterministic compilation. Human users can accept or reject individual specification changes before code generation.
- Limits: the prototype covers four core specification groups, unsupported analytic choices were excluded, cohort definition remains outside scope, and the study did not test actual researcher usability, completion time, or end-to-end scientific validity.

### Context only: EDPB — a contextual framework for anonymisation

- Direct sources: [consultation page](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/public-consultations/guidelines-022026-on-anonymisation_en), [version 1.0 PDF](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-07/edpb_guidelines_202602_anonymisation_v1_en_0.pdf)
- Date: adopted July 7, 2026; consultation opened July 8
- Stable ID and version: EDPB Guidelines 02/2026, version 1.0
- Internal score: 9/10
- Establishes: the draft tests anonymity through No Record Isolation, No Linkage, and No Inference. It offers a contextual approach based on the capabilities and information reasonably available to relevant entities, plus a deliberately more conservative simplified approach.
- Operational detail: the guidance says mixed datasets should be treated as personal if any included individuals remain identifiable and the portions cannot be separated. It calls for documentation and periodic reassessment and says a security incident can change the anonymity assessment.
- Why it matters: recipient, additional information, high-dimensional record structure, and release context are treated as part of the anonymity question rather than external paperwork.
- Limits: this is draft European GDPR guidance under consultation, not US HIPAA Expert Determination and not a final enforcement rule.

### Context only: Vallejo-Sanchez et al. — clinical-text de-identification needs task adaptation

- Direct source: [PubMed record and full indexed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42458625/)
- Date: July 15, 2026
- Identifiers: DOI 10.1186/s13326-026-00362-9; PMID 42458625
- Internal score: 8/10
- Establishes: the authors compared as-is task models, locally deployed prompt-based LLMs, and fine-tuned task models on the synthetic Spanish MEDDOCAN corpus and real obstetric EHR notes. As-is and prompt-based approaches reached macro F1 of 0.14–0.50; fine-tuned models reached up to 0.956.
- Why it matters: a generic generative model could make slight textual modifications that broke token alignment and information preservation. The result favors domain-specific adaptation and evaluation on real clinical text.
- Limits: entity-level precision, recall, and F1 are not a re-identification-risk determination. The study does not establish that a dataset is anonymous, HIPAA-de-identified, or safe for a particular recipient and release environment.

## What changed

The new signal is empirical evidence about failure boundaries. One TTE passed familiar balance diagnostics while its answer reversed with exposure definition; another failed positive and negative controls regardless of whether adjustment was conventional, semiparametric, or Transformer-based. In parallel, two agentic systems produced their strongest results only after narrowing the agent's role to structured extraction, deterministic tools, explicit diagnostics, and human certification.

The recurring theme is that data fitness and causal identification cannot be repaired by polish downstream. The EDPB draft and the Spanish de-identification study extend the same logic to privacy: success depends on the intended recipient, available linkage and inference means, real clinical text, and a documented risk test—not the mere use of a privacy technique or generic model.

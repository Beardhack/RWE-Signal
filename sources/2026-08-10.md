## Ranked evidence

### 1. Lu et al. - a two-stage LLM found EHR inconsistency candidates without a reference standard

- Direct source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22954)
- Publication date: July 24, 2026
- Stable ID: `arxiv:2607.22954`
- Content version: `v1`
- Internal score: 8/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. The source applies two frontier LLMs to real MIMIC-IV discharge summaries to detect data-quality defects that can affect EHR phenotyping and secondary clinical-research reuse.
- What the source establishes:
  - The pipeline used Gemini 2.5 Pro for open-ended candidate identification and Gemini 2.5 Flash for context-grounded verification.
  - It processed 3,000 randomly sampled MIMIC-IV-Note discharge summaries in three contiguous batches of 1,000.
  - It returned 3,460 candidate statement pairs and at least one candidate in 69.7% of admissions.
  - Candidate domains included demographics, allergies, procedures and devices, diagnoses and findings, laboratory and imaging, medications, and disposition or care planning.
  - Expert review identified representative meaningful discrepancies and recurring false or reconcilable patterns involving temporal progression, evolving diagnoses, and outpatient prescribing conventions.
  - The proposed ontology separates strict contradiction from ambiguity. The associated schema records document section, encounter phase, clinical domain, and contradiction axis such as presence, status, numeric value, timing, or laterality.
  - The authors explicitly state that the output was not fully adjudicated and that precision, sensitivity, prevalence, and comparative model performance cannot yet be claimed.
  - MIMIC-IV was used under its credentialed data agreement; Gemini ran through a PhysioNet-approved Vertex AI batch route, and the authors state that prompts and responses were not used to train Google's models.
- Why it matters: the pipeline could create a scalable queue for human data-quality review, but the headline flag rate is not an error rate. The study exposes a measurement gap that must be closed before detected contradictions alter research cohorts or variables.
- Limits: preprint; one de-identified critical-care note corpus; formative sampling; only a subset manually reviewed; no clinician-labeled gold standard; unknown false-negative and false-positive rates; no comparison with symbolic or dedicated temporal methods.
- Follow-up: build the proposed independently adjudicated corpus, report precision and sensitivity by domain and contradiction axis, and test whether structured timeline models resolve temporal false positives better than another general-purpose LLM.

### 2. Ishikane et al. - randomized evidence-review trial favored the LLM but missed superiority

- Direct source: [Open Forum Infectious Diseases](https://academic.oup.com/ofid/article/13/7/ofag401/8736362)
- Publication date: July 23, 2026
- Stable ID: `doi:10.1093/ofid/ofag401`
- Content version: `version-of-record`
- Trial registration: `UMIN000058346`
- Internal score: 7/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. The peer-reviewed randomized crossover trial tests an LLM-generated structured extraction as part of rapid evidence synthesis for emerging-infectious-disease decision-making.
- What the source establishes:
  - Five experienced reviewers, four physicians and one pharmacist, completed 20 task-level evaluations across mpox-related papers.
  - Nine tasks used a pre-generated OpenAI o3 structured summary and eleven used manual PDF review.
  - The system submitted the first 10,000 characters of each PDF to the model under a strict JSON schema. No fine-tuning or few-shot examples were used, and each paper was processed once before the trial.
  - Mean task time was 27.5 minutes with LLM assistance and 34.5 minutes manually.
  - The mixed-effects model estimated a 7.9-minute reduction, with a 95% confidence interval from 1.5 minutes slower to 17.3 minutes faster and `P = .099`.
  - Accuracy was 100% in both conditions, covering 9/9 LLM-assisted and 11/11 manual tasks. Intended meaning was assessed by one blinded infectious-disease specialist.
  - The LLM summarized; it did not search, screen, assess the article, or propose clinical actions. PDF downloading remained manual.
  - Repeated-prompt stability was not assessed because summaries were generated once and stored.
- Why it matters: the trial shifts evaluation from output plausibility to controlled measurement of researcher time. Its point estimate is operationally interesting, but the primary superiority test remained unresolved.
- Limits: five reviewers; six papers and twenty task evaluations; single-center expert group; single accuracy judge; only the first 10,000 PDF characters; no end-to-end review automation; no model-version or repeated-generation stability analysis.
- Follow-up: expand the crossover design across domains and reviewer experience levels, power it for smaller time effects, use independent dual adjudication, repeat generations, and test search, screening, extraction, and assessment as separate stages.

## What changed

Candidate generation is outpacing adjudication. The EHR pipeline can surface thousands of possible inconsistencies, and the evidence-review system can create a plausible time advantage, but neither result authorizes the final inference. The next validation target is the human decision that follows the AI artifact.

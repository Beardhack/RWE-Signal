# The AI read the chart. The chart was still incomplete.

**RWE-Signal - August 7, 2026 - Friday edition**

Three peer-reviewed studies show why an AI model's headline accuracy is only the beginning of RWE validation: the source record may be sparse, the prompt may encode clinical judgment, and the safest operating point may deliberately trade precision for sensitivity.

## High accuracy met low observability in rheumatoid arthritis

- **Source:** [Weisberg et al., JAMIA Open](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamiaopen/ooag152)
- **Date:** July 30, 2026
- **What happened:** A zero-shot GPT-4o workflow extracted the Clinical Disease Activity Index and its components from rheumatoid-arthritis notes with reported precision, recall, and F1 of 0.97.
- **Why it matters:** Only 160 of 1,038 seropositive patients had CDAI or components documented in an extractable form. The model performed well when the evidence existed; it did not make the wider cohort observable.
- **Evidence limits:** One academic EHR, sparse disease-activity documentation, and no proof that the workflow transports across sites or validates a downstream treatment-effect estimate.
- **Practical follow-up:** Report variable coverage alongside extraction accuracy before using an AI-derived measure in longitudinal RWE.

## Clinician-shaped prompts changed small-model extraction

- **Source:** [Corso et al., Communications Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43856-026-01790-5)
- **Date:** July 16, 2026
- **What happened:** Four locally deployable language models extracted treatment-relevant variables from records for 897 people with non-small-cell lung cancer. Clinical annotation and native-language prompts materially changed results.
- **Why it matters:** Average F1 ranged from 0.62 for LLaMA to 0.24 for MedLLaMA. ECOG extraction reached 0.97 accuracy with clinical annotation, while T-stage remained poor. Performance belonged to a model-task-prompt combination, not to the model name alone.
- **Evidence limits:** One cancer setting, a small clinician sample, difficult implicit staging language, and limited external validation.
- **Practical follow-up:** Validate each field separately and preserve prompt, annotation, language, and model-version provenance.

## Pharmacovigilance exposed the operating-point tradeoff

- **Source:** [Fong et al., PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42490993/)
- **Date:** July 16, 2026
- **What happened:** An LLM-augmented workflow screened 20,123 documents from 46 patients receiving anti-amyloid therapies. It reached 100% sensitivity and 89.7% positive predictive value; standard expert review reached 88.6% and 100%, respectively.
- **Why it matters:** The model did not simply replace expert review. It moved the balance toward finding every adjudicated safety event at the cost of extra false positives.
- **Evidence limits:** Preliminary, retrospective, single-system evidence from 46 patients.
- **Practical follow-up:** Measure false-positive review burden, time saved, transportability, and drift before operational use.

## Friday synthesis

This week's evidence repeatedly hit three ceilings: whether the clinical fact was observable, whether the derived variable was specified consistently, and whether a human-auditable workflow preserved the path from source evidence to result. The credible pattern is not autonomous evidence generation. It is explicit provenance, task-specific validation, versioned logic, and human review where error costs differ.

## Source health

Alternate primary records were used for verification; social indexing was used only for discovery.

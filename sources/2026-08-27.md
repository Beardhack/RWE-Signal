# Research notes - 2026-08-27

Three newly public records passed the hard intersection and novelty gates. ## Ranked evidence

### 1. A Multimodal Foundation Model for Longitudinal Patient Representation and Scalable Insight Generation in Oncology

- Direct source: [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24688) and [complete HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24688)
- Publication date: August 25, 2026 (announced in the August 26 arXiv cycle)
- Stable ID: `arxiv:2608.24688`; story ID: `ofm-longitudinal-oncology-foundation-model`; content version: `v1`
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes as a foundation-model workflow trained and evaluated on longitudinal real-world oncology evidence**. It integrates clinical trajectories, DNA, RNA, and H&E pathology from 1.67 million patients, then evaluates prognostic and comparative-treatment tasks.
- What the source establishes: more than one million patients were used for training; patient-level partitions separated training, validation, and testing. Overall-survival AUC was 0.774 versus 0.563 for expert-curated baseline features. Across 11 comparative-treatment cohorts, treatment-benefit ranking was stronger in 9 and the pooled scale-normalized AUTOC statistic was 4.61 versus 1.38.
- Why it matters: a reusable longitudinal patient representation carried more signal than static curated features across several oncology evidence tasks and supplied an evidence-grounded temporal-graph interpretation layer.
- Evidence limits: vendor-authored living preprint; retrospective proprietary data; no independent replication, prospective validation, clinically validated biomarker, or established causal treatment effect. The authors state that the benchmarks compare representation quality.

### 2. Improvement of Clinical Practice Guideline Appraisal by Human Experts and AI Agents by Using Structured Guidance

- Direct source: [peer-reviewed JMIR article](https://www.jmir.org/2026/1/e96002)
- Publication date: August 26, 2026; DOI `10.2196/96002`; PMID `42647856`
- Stable ID: `doi:10.2196/96002`; story ID: `structured-guidance-ai-guideline-appraisal`; content version: `journal-v1`
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes as a retrieval-augmented AI-agent workflow for evidence appraisal and synthesis**. Two LLM agents appraised 227 clinical-practice guidelines using AGREE II and RIGHT, with human consensus and an external six-guideline set as comparisons.
- What the source establishes: workbook guidance increased agent-human ICC from 0.613 to 0.709 for DeepSeek-R1 and from 0.629 to 0.687 for o1-mini. In the external set, DeepSeek-R1's ICC was 0.711 and mean appraisal time was 5.44 minutes, versus 11.18 minutes for humans.
- Why it matters: expert-authored structure improved model agreement more than an unconstrained appraisal prompt and created a reproducible human-in-the-loop evidence-review pattern.
- Evidence limits: the external validation covered six ACL-reconstruction guidelines; agreement within a shared framework is not independent truth; unsupported references and overinterpretation still occurred.

### 3. TrialScout links published results to trial registrations using a large language model

- Direct source: [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42648652/) and [open preprint/version history](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.15.26348383v2.full)
- Electronic publication date: August 26, 2026; accepted August 20; DOI `10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112484`; PMID `42648652`
- Stable ID: `doi:10.1016/j.jclinepi.2026.112484`; story ID: `trialscout-registry-publication-linking`; content version: `journal-v1`
- Internal score: 8/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes as an LLM evidence-provenance workflow over clinical-trial registrations and result publications**. It links ClinicalTrials.gov records with PubMed-indexed result papers and measures against human-coded matches.
- What the source establishes: sensitivity 92.5% and specificity 81.2% versus human coders. In 200 disagreements reviewed manually, 123 (61.5%) reflected human errors. Applied to 9,600 completed or terminated trials, TrialScout found result publications for 6,110 (63.6%).
- Why it matters: evidence-synthesis and trial-transparency teams gain a scalable way to restore links between registrations and published results.
- Evidence limits: no error-free gold standard; the journal full text was not openly rendered during this run, so peer-reviewed metadata and abstract were verified through PubMed and compared with the open v2 preprint.

- Coala is useful agentic bioinformatics infrastructure, but the source did not demonstrate a concrete health-evidence application.
- Current EHR prediction, triage, imaging, and clinical-decision-support papers did not perform a qualifying evidence-lifecycle task.
- General AI-enabled RWE reviews and policy pieces did not establish a material agent, LLM, or foundation-model workflow in the same development.
- Previously published THESEUS, Future Querying, RegCheck, PHO-Agents, and other known artifacts were rejected as duplicates or dated background.

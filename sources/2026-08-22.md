## Ranked evidence

### 1. An AI System for Autonomous Algorithm Evolution in Drug Development

- Direct sources: [bioRxiv v1](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.16.745117v1) and [Apache-2.0 repository](https://github.com/GAIR-NLP/DrugEvolve)
- Publication date: August 20, 2026
- DOI: `10.64898/2026.08.16.745117`
- Stable ID: `doi:10.64898/2026.08.16.745117`
- Content version: `v1`
- Authors: Zhou et al.
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes through agentic scientific evidence infrastructure**. A three-role LLM system autonomously designs, implements, evaluates, and refines task-specific algorithms across the drug-development evidence lifecycle, including preclinical and clinical-trial tasks.
- What the primary sources establish:
  - DrugEvolve separates Researcher, Engineer, and Analyst roles and iterates from scientific proposal to executable code, objective evaluation, analysis, and memory.
  - The evaluation covers 11 tasks and 120 benchmark test sets spanning target identification, drug discovery, preclinical study, and clinical trial.
  - The repository exposes before-and-after implementations for cancer-gene modules, gene-disease association, druggable sites, docking, drug-target interaction, molecular generation, peptide binders, ADMET, animal toxicity, drug-drug interaction, and side-effect prediction.
  - Candidate code, scores, failures, analyses, parents, lineage, and reusable experience are preserved; experiments run locally with configurable timeouts and human preflight confirmation.
- Why it matters: the artifact turns scientific-agent orchestration into inspectable algorithmic infrastructure and provides a concrete pattern for cumulative improvement across the development pipeline rather than a single isolated prediction task.
- Evidence limits: this is a preprint and benchmark demonstration, not a prospective drug program or regulatory evidence submission. The abstract reports substantial enhancement but does not expose one harmonized effect size across heterogeneous tasks.

### 2. BioPathfinder: Evidence-guided multi-agent platform enables hypothesis discovery for CAR-T engineering

- Direct sources: [bioRxiv v2](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.15.738646v2) and [code release](https://github.com/TeammateDownloadGenshin/BioPathfinder/releases/tag/v1.0.0-rc3)
- Substantive update date: July 28, 2026
- DOI: `10.64898/2026.07.15.738646`
- Stable ID: `doi:10.64898/2026.07.15.738646`
- Content version: `v2`
- Authors: Shubing Wang et al.
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. Role-specialized LLM agents work over a provenance-aware resource connecting publications, patient scRNA-seq, and clinical metadata to generate and prioritize falsifiable CAR-T mechanisms for computational and experimental validation.
- What the primary source establishes:
  - The resource connects clinical studies, patient single-cell data, and metadata with provenance.
  - Agents generate, review, and prioritize dataset-aware hypotheses; virtual perturbation highlighted KLRC1, KLRD1, and KLRG1.
  - Experts selected KLRC1/NKG2A, and in vitro and in vivo chronic-stimulation experiments showed that NKG2A blockade enhanced antitumor activity and persistence-associated readouts.
  - Version 2 substantially clarified the workflow and results and updated figures, methods, supplementary materials, author list, references, and code availability.
- Why it matters: this is a concrete bridge from fragmented patient-derived evidence through agentic hypothesis formation to laboratory validation.
- Evidence limits: it remains a preprint. Experimental validation is not prospective clinical validation, and the generality of the result beyond the curated CAR-T corpus remains to be established.

### 3. Addressing challenges in agentic retrieval of structured data from biomedical databases

- Direct sources: [bioRxiv v2](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.25.720782v2) and [public repository](https://github.com/abhi1238/biochirp)
- Substantive update date: August 9, 2026
- DOI: `10.64898/2026.04.25.720782`
- Stable ID: `doi:10.64898/2026.04.25.720782`
- Content version: `v2`
- Authors: Abhishek Halder et al.
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes through agentic scientific evidence infrastructure**. BioChirp uses coordinated language models to interpret questions and resolve biomedical terminology, then deterministic planning and execution to retrieve structured evidence across curated disease, gene, variant, phenotype, pathway, and therapeutic databases.
- What the primary source establishes:
  - Across 70 queries, five runs, and three backends, median cross-run Jaccard similarity was 1.0.
  - Across 910 database-question evaluations spanning 10 databases, the system retrieved at least one record in 85.4% of cases and produced a fully correct answer in 51.1%, compared with 44.6% and 17.4% for agentic NL2SQL.
  - Version 2 substantially expands cross-database evaluation and failure analysis and clarifies the interpretation-execution architecture, entity resolution, Steiner-tree planning, and MCP interface.
- Why it matters: reproducible biomedical evidence retrieval becomes an explicit system property rather than an assumed consequence of using an agent.
- Evidence limits: this is a preprint and database-retrieval benchmark, not a patient-level RWE study. Retrieval completeness is distinct from answer correctness, and the reported guarantees apply within configured operational limits.

### 4. Quality-aware multi-source data fusion and enhancement for Medical Concept Normalization using Large Language Models

- Direct source: [primary PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42628772/)
- Code: [DataFusion4MCN](https://github.com/yhZHOU515/DataFusion4MCN)
- Publication date: August 21, 2026
- DOI: `10.1016/j.jbi.2026.105093`
- PMID: `42628772`
- Stable ID: `doi:10.1016/j.jbi.2026.105093`
- Content version: `version-of-record`
- Authors: Yuhan Zhou et al.
- Internal score: 10/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. Gemini evaluates and enhances six real-world patient-language datasets, generates controlled phrase variants, and fuses SNOMED-CT mappings to improve downstream medical concept normalization for pharmacovigilance and patient-record analysis.
- What the primary record establishes:
  - The six datasets are AskAPatient, CADEC, COMETA, PsyTAR, TwADR-S, and TwiMed, each derived from social media and mapped to SNOMED-CT.
  - Phrase counts increased 172.1% after augmentation and 450.0% after fusion.
  - SapBERT, KNN-BioEL, and KRISSBERT gained up to 38% over single-source baselines on accuracy, recall, precision, and F1.
- Why it matters: the study makes data quality and coverage part of the AI pipeline before downstream evidence extraction begins.
- Evidence limits: the complete publisher article was not publicly accessible; reporting uses the primary PubMed abstract and metadata. The evaluation uses six social-media-derived datasets and downstream concept-linking models, not clinical outcomes.

### 5. Development of an LLM pipeline exceeding physician-documented cardiovascular risk scores under routine clinical conditions

- Direct source: [Oxford Academic accepted manuscript](https://academic.oup.com/ehjdh/advance-article/doi/10.1093/ehjdh/ztag124/8746371)
- Publication date: July 28, 2026
- DOI: `10.1093/ehjdh/ztag124`
- PMID: `42626630`
- Stable ID: `doi:10.1093/ehjdh/ztag124`
- Content version: `accepted-manuscript`
- Authors: Tobias Roeschl et al.
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. Six LLMs extract variables from routine reports in two real-world cardiovascular cohorts, while deterministic calculation produces HAS-BLED, CHA2DS2-VASc, and EuroSCORE II results against expert-adjudicated ground truth.
- What the primary source establishes:
  - The retrospective single-center study included 179 atrial-fibrillation ablation patients and 76 severe-aortic-stenosis heart-team patients from January 2020 through July 2023.
  - Mean Krippendorff alpha was 0.78 for the pipeline versus 0.32 standalone, 0.39 with RAG, and 0.31 for physician-documented scores.
  - Mean RMSE was 0.89 for the pipeline versus 5.81, 1.85, and 1.34 respectively.
- Why it matters: the architecture assigns language interpretation to the model and arithmetic to deterministic code, making the route from unstructured real-world data to a clinical score inspectable.
- Evidence limits: the accepted manuscript may change after typesetting. This is one quaternary center and 255 patients; agreement with adjudicated scores is not a prospective outcome study.

### 6. Clinical Trial Source Document Verification Using a Large Language Model

- Direct source: [open full text in PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13474625/)
- Publication date: August 5, 2026
- DOI: `10.1016/j.jacadv.2026.103108`
- PMID: `42556211`
- PMCID: `PMC13474625`
- Stable ID: `doi:10.1016/j.jacadv.2026.103108`
- Content version: `version-of-record`
- Authors: Simran Makker et al.
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. An o4-mini workflow compares paired trial eCRFs with participant medical records to verify 24 baseline medical-history variables in the multicenter INVESTED trial.
- What the primary source establishes:
  - The trial enrolled 5,260 patients at 157 North American sites; 293 had submitted source records, and 119 English-language records formed the held-out primary validation set.
  - Across 2,856 variable-level observations, LLM agreement with a prespecified reference reviewer was 96%; inappropriate verification occurred in fewer than 2% of entries.
  - The proposed operating model is first-pass verification followed by human review of discordant or low-confidence items.
- Why it matters: source-document verification is a concrete, labor-intensive evidence-governance task, and the study tests a human-led automation path on actual multicenter trial records.
- Evidence limits: the reference review was not adjudicated as ground truth. The evaluation covers one trial, English records, and baseline history variables; longitudinal outcomes, adverse events, medications, endpoints, and lower-quality scans require separate validation.

- Authors: Ashvin Gupta, Denys Prociuk, Alessandra Russo, and Brendan C. Delaney
- Internal score: 8/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes through executable evidence infrastructure**. A stepwise LLM transformation turns two NICE cancer guidelines into inspectable executable rules and tests patient-specific recommendations on 20 pancreatic-cancer vignettes.

- Evidence limits: only two guidelines and 20 vignettes were evaluated. Vignettes are not real patient records, so this qualifies as executable evidence infrastructure rather than a direct RWD study.

### 8. Performance of large language models in data extraction for evidence synthesis: A systematic review

- Direct source: [primary PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42501879/)
- Publication date: July 25, 2026
- DOI: `10.1016/j.jbi.2026.105086`
- PMID: `42501879`
- Stable ID: `doi:10.1016/j.jbi.2026.105086`
- Content version: `version-of-record`
- Authors: Ravi Shankar, Amaevia Lim, and Xu Qian
- Internal score: 8/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. The review evaluates LLM accuracy, reliability, efficiency, and implementation strategy for extracting study data inside systematic evidence synthesis.
- What the primary record establishes:
  - Twenty-seven studies met inclusion criteria; overall accuracy ranged from 47% to 99.9%.
  - Categorical and string variables reached 74%-96%, compared with 47%-88% for numerical data.
  - Claude 3.5 Sonnet reached 91.0% in an assistive workflow versus 89.0% for human-only extraction; omissions accounted for 60%-74% of errors, hallucination for 0.08%-6%.
  - Reported time savings ranged from 33%-87%, although most studies did not quantify efficiency.
- Why it matters: the review supports dual extraction with human verification and structured outputs as a practical evidence-synthesis pattern.
- Evidence limits: heterogeneity precluded meta-analysis, most studies did not quantify efficiency, and gaps remained in inference settings and model-version reporting.

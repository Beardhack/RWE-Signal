# The agent ran the study. Humans rebuilt the cohort.

**RWE-Signal - August 9, 2026 - Daily edition**

A governed AI orchestrated a 286,422-person hospital GWAS without receiving patient rows, but human review still had to repair the hypertension cohort before the analysis matched expert results.

## Controlled access did not settle the cohort definition

- **Source:** [Huang et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11084)
- **Date:** July 13, 2026
- **What happened:** An agent planned and executed a hospital-linked hypertension GWAS inside a governed environment. Its early phenotype placed 3,950 people in a case group that the expert definition treated as controls; a human-led medication audit and directed reconciliation were required before the workflow reproduced established loci.
- **Why it matters:** Access controls can keep raw records inside an approved boundary and make execution auditable, but they cannot decide whether a clinical phenotype is scientifically valid.
- **Evidence limits:** One-institution preprint, months of iterative engineering, and a replication rather than a novel genetic discovery.

## Pipeline-building agents led narrowly on four real-data tasks

- **Source:** [Wang et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05375)
- **Date:** August 5, 2026
- **What happened:** DoctorAgents used specialized agents and shared memory to generate, repair, and optimize complete machine-learning pipelines across three MIMIC-IV tasks and a longitudinal rheumatoid-arthritis cohort.
- **Why it matters:** The system shows that agent teams can automate more than model selection, including preprocessing, debugging, and iterative feature development.
- **Evidence limits:** Small internal margins, no external institution, modest rheumatoid-arthritis performance, and feature quality judged partly by another language model.

## Small adapters moved between sites; patient notes did not

- **Source:** [Liu et al., JAMIA Open](https://academic.oup.com/jamiaopen/article/9/4/ooag139/8738251)
- **Date:** July 20, 2026
- **What happened:** Sequential low-rank fine-tuning across three institutions approached centralized training for mobility-status extraction and impairment classification while keeping 600 clinical notes at their source sites.
- **Why it matters:** Task-specific adaptation of an 8B model outperformed an untuned 70B model and reduced the need to pool raw clinical text.
- **Evidence limits:** Three sites in one region, fixed training order, modest sample, no formal privacy audit, and acknowledged leakage risk from transferred weights.

## What changed

AI agents can now orchestrate substantial evidence workflows under real access constraints. The new evidence also marks their boundary: humans still own phenotype definitions, transportability, and privacy validation. A controlled architecture can enforce who sees the data; it cannot determine whether the resulting evidence is correct.

## Source health

arXiv, Europe PMC, Oxford Academic, FDA, ISPOR, and institutional pages were accessible. PubMed intermittently presented a reCAPTCHA interstitial, and public LinkedIn indexing remained incomplete. Primary full text and stable metadata records were used for verification; social indexing was used only for discovery.

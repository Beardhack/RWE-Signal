# BALMS mapped the agent architecture for longitudinal mental-health sensing

**RWE-Signal - August 30, 2026 - Sunday edition**

A new EMNLP 2026 benchmark tests how prompt-, memory-, and tool-based LLM agents turn longitudinal phone and wearable histories into wellbeing scores and evidence-grounded rationales.

## Selective memory made longer histories useful

- **Source:** [BALMS arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27219)
- **Date:** August 27, 2026
- **What changed:** Four agent systems and five model backbones were evaluated on three de-identified real-world sensing datasets. The best observed agent beat a simple mean predictor on DiversityOne and PMData; retrieval improved as more PMData history became available, while compact smartwatch features frequently matched or surpassed fuller multimodal inputs.
- **Evidence limits:** The study is retrospective and not a clinical deployment. One dataset contributes only 16 participants, two datasets use restricted evaluation subsets, and rationale quality is judged by an LLM rather than clinicians or participants.

## The signal

BALMS turns longitudinal health-agent design into a reproducible comparison. Its strongest result is not one universal winner: it is a blueprint for pairing capable backbones with selective memory, interpretable features, and tool contracts that match the source schema. That makes patient-generated health data more usable without treating longer context as an automatic improvement.

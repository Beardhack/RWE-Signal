# Research notes - 2026-08-31

## Editorial line

**Headline:** FedEHR-Agents let hospitals share modeling intelligence without pooling patient records.

One previously unreported preprint passed the hard intersection, novelty, evidence, and timeliness gates. FedEHR-Agents reframes cross-hospital collaboration as the exchange of evaluated modeling experience: autonomous agents preprocess local EHRs, build and assess prediction pipelines, retain local memory, and share refined prompts plus structured evidence for server-side synthesis.

## Ranked evidence

### 1. FedEHR-Agents: Federated Agentic Optimization for Automated EHR Modeling

- Direct source: [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27856) and [complete public HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27856).
- First public date: August 28, 2026 at 03:02:10 UTC.
- Authors and affiliation: Jun Bai, Ruilin Wang, and Yue Li; McGill University and Mila - Quebec AI Institute.
- Stable ID: `arxiv:2608.27856`; story ID: `fedehr-agents-federated-ehr-modeling-2026`; content version: `v1`.
- Internal score: 9/10.
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes as an LLM-agent framework that autonomously preprocesses longitudinal multi-hospital EHR data, constructs and evaluates clinical prediction models, and transfers evidence-backed modeling experience across distributed real-world datasets.** The system operates directly on named RWD and performs concrete evidence-lifecycle work.
- What the source establishes:
  - Each hospital runs an autonomous agent with data-preprocessing and model-development modules, historical memory, task-specific evaluation, and TextGrad prompt refinement.
  - Raw patient-level EHR data, local prediction models, and historical memory stay within each hospital. Refined executable prompts and structured modeling evidence - validation performance, feature importance, feature statistics, model preferences, and optimization trends - are sent to the federated server.
  - The server performs evidence-guided experience aggregation and returns global meta-prompts for the next local modeling round.
  - Evaluation uses the public eICU Collaborative Research Database, treating hospitals as separate clients, across four tasks: 48-hour mortality, four-hour acute respiratory failure, prolonged length of stay, and sepsis.
  - The main configuration uses three hospitals, five communication rounds, and three random seeds. GPT-5 mini is the default backbone; robustness tests also use GPT-4o and GPT-5.
  - Compared with the same agent working locally, FedEHR-Agents raises AUPRC from 0.218 to 0.236 for mortality, 0.171 to 0.194 for acute respiratory failure, 0.483 to 0.532 for length of stay, and 0.268 to 0.316 for sepsis. These are relative gains of 8.3%, 13.5%, 10.1%, and 17.9%.
  - It also exceeds simple prompt averaging on every task. Removing the evidence-guided aggregation component reduces mortality AUPRC from 0.236 to 0.221.
  - Scaling from three to 20 hospitals raises FedEHR-Agents AUPRC from 0.236 to 0.252 for mortality, 0.194 to 0.236 for acute respiratory failure, 0.532 to 0.553 for length of stay, and 0.316 to 0.341 for sepsis.
- Limits: this is a non-peer-reviewed preprint and retrospective benchmark, not a live federation among health systems. The source standardizes eICU tables before agent processing, and its hospital-client simulations do not establish prospective clinical utility. The shared experience excludes raw records but may still encode local data characteristics; the authors explicitly say the design is privacy-aware rather than a formal privacy guarantee. No differential privacy, secure aggregation, trusted execution environment, or long-term deployment evaluation is demonstrated.
- Commercial context: the academic study evaluates three commercial LLM backbones but reports no product deployment.

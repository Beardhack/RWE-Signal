## Ranked evidence

### 1. Jiang et al. — treatment-context medication evidence

- Direct source: [arXiv 2608.01570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01570)
- Date: August 3, 2026
- Stable ID: `arxiv:2608.01570`
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. The primary source materially uses an LLM to construct an auditable reference layer for medication evidence derived from real-world clinical notes and compares that evidence with structured EHR history.
- What the source establishes:
  - The frozen cohort included 761 patients, 11,812 eligible visits, and 22,483 manifest notes.
  - Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, deployed locally with deterministic decoding and schema-constrained JSON, proposed note-level medication mentions for reference construction.
  - Humans audited 3,188 of 27,752 reference rows in a difficult-case-enriched queue; a separate stratified random sample covered 1,000 previously unaudited rows.
  - On 5,403 held-out mention rows, exact canonical agreement improved from 0.7226 with surface-exact matching to 0.8429 after lexical cleanup and curated alias mapping.
  - Random-audit canonical-label agreement was 0.9210 among evaluable valid mentions, while treatment-action attribution was 0.5326.
  - Cumulative overlap with structured medication history rose from 16.44% for same-visit exact matching to 55.17% for same-visit semantic matching, 90.34% within 30 days, and 96.03% across any history.
  - In 100 manually reviewed examples from a strict OMOP-backed no-overlap group, 80 reflected note-side mapping failure, six structured-side undercapture, seven extraction/reference noise, and seven candidate note-only evidence.
- Why it matters: it demonstrates that a binary match test can conflate mapping, terminology, timing, extraction, and source-coverage problems that have different implications for RWE variables.
- Limits: preprint; private single-center environment; project-specific canonical labels and categories; incomplete manual annotation; institution-shaped alias resource; normalization performance is not end-to-end extraction validity or proof of medication use.
- Follow-up: quantify each discordance mechanism before treating cross-source mismatch as endpoint missingness or data-source failure.

### 2. Yang et al. — SHARE human–agent phenotyping

- Direct source: [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.16.26358271v1.full)
- Date: July 16, 2026
- Stable ID: `doi:10.64898/2026.07.16.26358271`
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. The primary source evaluates a human–LLM agent workflow for producing rheumatoid-arthritis disease-activity phenotypes from real-world EHR notes specifically to support RWE treatment studies.
- What the source establishes:
  - The cohort included 3,167 patients and 132,852 notes in a multi-institutional Mass General Brigham EHR environment.
  - Experts initially labeled 626 notes from 273 patients. Human–AI adjudication revised 127 labels and added 60 notes, producing a 686-note co-produced reference.
  - The final agent's mean absolute error improved from 0.406 to 0.291; its ambiguity flag matched expert ambiguity designations with 92.1% accuracy.
  - The agent labeled 101,691 notes across the cohort.
  - A tiered configuration using a lower-cost model for extraction and a stronger model for reasoning matched the GPT-5.4 high-reasoning configuration while reducing estimated cost by 69% and compute time by 70%.
- Why it matters: it turns disagreement into an explicit review queue and preserves evidence, rationale, confidence, and ambiguity instead of treating model or initial human labels as ground truth.
- Limits: preprint; internal validation; one health-system environment, one disease, and one phenotype; co-produced labels are not an independent external gold standard; downstream treatment-effect validity was not tested.
- Follow-up: externally validate the guideline, ambiguity triggers, and error distribution before reusing the agent across diseases or sites.

## What changed

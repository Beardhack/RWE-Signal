# The missing medication was often a mapping problem

**RWE-Signal — August 5, 2026 — Daily edition**

Two preprints show why labels derived from clinical text should be treated as evidence artifacts rather than clerical outputs.

## Medication mismatch changes under semantic and temporal checks

- **Source:** [Jiang et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01570)
- **Date:** August 3, 2026
- **Topic:** LLM-assisted medication evidence characterization in EHR data
- **What happened:** A locally deployed LLM bootstrapped a note-grounded reference set; targeted and random human review bounded label quality; deterministic normalization was tested on held-out patients; note-derived medications were compared with structured history across semantic and temporal windows.
- **Why it matters:** Exact same-visit overlap was only 16.44%, but cumulative overlap reached 55.17% with same-visit semantic matching, 90.34% within 30 days, and 96.03% across any history. A failed exact match cannot be interpreted as a single kind of missing data.
- **Evidence limits:** This is a preprint from one private clinical environment. The label space and aliases are project-specific, only part of the reference set was manually audited, and treatment-action attribution remained substantially weaker than medication-name normalization.
- **Practical follow-up:** Before using medication discordance in an RWE endpoint or covariate, what share is explained by terminology, timing, mapping coverage, extraction error, and genuine source undercapture?

## SHARE turns phenotype disagreement into targeted expert review

- **Source:** [Yang et al., medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.16.26358271v1.full)
- **Date:** July 16, 2026
- **Topic:** Human–agent co-learning for RWE phenotyping
- **What happened:** Clinical experts and an evidence-grounded agent labeled rheumatoid-arthritis disease activity from multi-institutional EHR notes. Disagreements were adjudicated, rules were refined, and the agent was scaled across the cohort.
- **Why it matters:** Adjudication changed 127 of 626 initial human labels and added 60 missed notes. The final agent's mean absolute error improved from 0.406 to 0.291; a tiered model configuration matched the high-reasoning setup while reducing estimated cost by 69% and compute time by 70%.
- **Evidence limits:** This is an internally evaluated preprint in one health-system environment and one phenotype. External transportability and downstream treatment-effect validity remain untested.
- **Practical follow-up:** Which ambiguity and disagreement patterns should trigger expert review before a phenotype is reused across sites or studies?

## What changed

The useful frontier is moving from model output to reviewable evidence production. Both studies preserve supporting text, expose uncertainty, use people for adjudication, and avoid treating the LLM as ground truth.

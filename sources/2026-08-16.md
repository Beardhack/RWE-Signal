## Editorial line

**Headline:** Agentic validation reached across oncology submissions.

Four previously unreported sources published on August 13-14 show frontier AI moving deeper into evidence production and quality control. ## Ranked evidence

### 1. CAVE-Onc: Graph-constrained agentic validation for cross-domain contradictions in CDISC oncology submissions

- Direct sources: [PLOS One article](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0350376), [complete PMC text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13475866/), [open benchmark repository](https://github.com/yanmingyu92/CAVE-Onc-Benchmark), and [OSF preregistration](https://osf.io/fx2ky)
- Electronic publication date: August 14, 2026
- Stable ID: `doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0350376`
- PMID: `42599988`
- PMCID: `PMC13475866`
- Content version: `version-of-record`
- Author: Jaime Yan, Harrisburg University of Science and Technology
- Internal score: 10/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. A graph-constrained LangGraph agent and declarative validation layer test clinical-trial evidence records across CDISC oncology domains, including mapped participant-level data from two real oncology trials.
- What the primary sources establish:
  - CAVE-Onc converts XPT submission datasets into an RDF knowledge graph. Layer 1 contains 111 SHACL shapes: 85 ported CORE rules, 8 RECIST 1.1 derivation shapes, and 18 cross-domain SHACL-SPARQL constraints.
  - Layer 3 uses a constrained CaveAgent to query target-lesion, non-target-lesion, and new-lesion records, apply the RECIST Table 7 decision matrix, compare the expected overall response with the submitted value, and emit a trace. The primary detector is deterministic by design; generative models were evaluated as verification and explanation layers.
  - In the preregistered 20-archetype construction validation, CAVE detected 20/20 contradictions. CORE detected 8/20 and Pinnacle 21 FDA detected 6/20. On the 10 cross-domain RECIST contradictions outside the CORE rule class, CAVE detected 10/10 while both comparison validators detected 0/10.
  - The Layer 1-only ablation detected 19/20; the agent contributed the sole additional RECIST Table 7 contradiction.
  - Existing shapes detected 3/5 held-out archetypes, including 2/5 through the intended mechanism.
  - For real-data transfer, two Project Data Sphere oncology trials were mapped to SDTM. The engine remained specific on unmutated data at 0.06 and 0.09 archetype flags per subject, then detected 10/11 applicable mutations in Synta and 16/18 in CA012. These are injected-contradiction transfer results, not prevalence estimates.
  - The open MIT-licensed repository contains code, benchmark data, committed evaluation artifacts, tests, mapping logic, a hash-chained audit store, and reproducibility commands. Its faster Oxigraph backend processed approximately 4,900 subjects in about 24 seconds of query time in the reported scale test.
- Why it matters: CAVE-Onc gives an evidence-validation agent a typed, graph-shaped view across submission domains. It preserves the structural validators that sponsors already use while adding a reproducible layer for clinical contradictions that only appear when lesion, response, exposure, adverse-event, and timing records are read together.
- Evidence limits: the headline 20/20 is construction validation because shapes were authored with knowledge of the injected patterns. The real trials were mapped into SDTM rather than raw regulatory submissions, and transfer was evaluated by injecting contradictions rather than labeling naturally occurring ones. Scope is RECIST 1.1 oncology. The agent's unique primary detection contribution is one archetype and uses deterministic lookup. The hash chain is a Part 11 foundation, not full Part 11 compliance.
- Follow-up: blinded evaluation on raw submissions; broader therapeutic areas and response criteria; more agent-dependent contradiction classes; protocol-specific configuration; larger expert panels; and full authentication, access-control, and validation engineering.

### 2. Making Broad Evidence Synthesis Feasible: An LLM Screening Agent for Meta-Analyses Applied To Suicide Prevention

- Direct source: [medRxiv v1](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.12.26360335v1)
- Publication date: August 14, 2026
- Stable ID: `doi:10.64898/2026.08.12.26360335`
- Content version: `v1`
- Authors: Daniel Dobin et al., Johns Hopkins University and Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. An LLM screening agent performs title-and-abstract eligibility decisions for a prospective suicide-prevention meta-analysis and two external systematic reviews, a named evidence-synthesis task.
- What the primary source establishes:
  - ScreenAgent returns structured include/exclude decisions. Records selected for inclusion receive a second cascade pass with a higher-effort LLM; human reviewers remain the final arbiters.
  - Internal validation covered a prospective meta-analysis with 201,064 records. The agent identified 43 of 44 eligible studies: sensitivity 97.7% (95% CI 88.2%-99.6%), specificity 98.0%, and full-corpus workload reduction 99.4%.
  - Full-corpus model cost was $855.91, or 0.43 US cents per record.
  - Agreement between the agent and human consensus exceeded human-versus-human agreement in the reported comparison: Cohen kappa 0.75 versus 0.64 and percent agreement 97.3% versus 95.4%.
  - On two external published reviews, automatically tuned prompts produced five-fold cross-validated sensitivity of 95.9% and 97.4%, with workload reductions of 97.4% and 98.4%.
- Why it matters: broad evidence searches can capture upstream interventions that narrow searches miss, but a 200,000-record result set is not manually tractable. ScreenAgent turns broad capture into a measurable cascade while keeping inclusion decisions reviewable.
- Evidence limits: this is a preprint. The internal set contained 44 eligible studies, and the two external results used automatic prompt tuning plus cross-validation rather than prospective deployment. Performance may not transfer to other evidence domains or databases. Data and analytic code are available only on request, and cost depends on model and pricing.
- Follow-up: prospective external reviews in other therapeutic areas; independent reproduction; open prompts and code; study-level analysis of the single internal miss; calibration of review-specific sensitivity estimates; and reviewer-time measurement.

### 3. Clinical Utility of LLM-assisted Chart Review for the Detection of Bleeding Events

- Direct sources: [peer-reviewed article DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tracli.2026.07.005), [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42600978/)
- Electronic publication date: August 14, 2026
- Stable ID: `doi:10.1016/j.tracli.2026.07.005`
- PMID: `42600978`
- Content version: `version-of-record`
- Authors: Merijn C. Reuland et al., Amsterdam UMC collaborators
- Internal score: 9/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. GPT-4o-mini screens unstructured EHR notes to build an adjudicated ICU bleeding-event dataset for retrospective outcomes research.
- What the primary source establishes:
  - The retrospective high-bleeding-risk ICU cohort contained 149 patients and 36,490 notes.
  - A secure GPT-4o-mini workflow used structured JSON prompts. Candidate events were adjudicated to create a verified reference set of 654 true bleeding events.
  - Initial manual review identified 66 true events. The LLM identified 647 of the 654 final true events, including 588 not captured by the initial manual review; the paper reports a 90.1% incremental detection yield.
  - The LLM missed 7 true events. Of 739 LLM-identified candidates, 85 were not confirmed, a false-positive rate of 11.5%.
  - Site classification was correct in 42% of cases and severity classification in 65%.
- Why it matters: the model served as a high-recall discovery layer over a volume of notes that makes exhaustive chart review difficult. Expert adjudication converted the additional candidates into a richer event dataset rather than treating model output as final truth.
- Evidence limits: this is a single-centre, 149-patient cohort. The verified reference set was expanded after model-assisted discovery, so the comparison is not a fully independent locked-reference evaluation. False positives and limited site/severity accuracy make adjudication necessary. No external validation or formal reviewer-time comparison was reported in the abstract.
- Follow-up: locked-reference external validation; prospective multisite use; human-time and cost analysis; improved site/severity extraction; and evaluation of whether richer event capture changes treatment-safety estimates.

### 4. Diagnostic accuracy of EMR retrieval methods and an LLM for cardiovascular events

- Direct sources: [BMJ Open article](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2025-116133), [complete PMC text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13475313/)
- Electronic publication date: August 13, 2026
- Stable ID: `doi:10.1136/bmjopen-2025-116133`
- PMID: `42595369`
- PMCID: `PMC13475313`
- Content version: `version-of-record`
- Authors: Omar Ibrahim et al., Mayo Clinic
- Internal score: 10/10
- `ai_rwe_intersection`: **passes**. An EMR-integrated zero-shot LLM identifies adjudicated cardiovascular outcomes in two retrospective cohorts across three sites and is compared with code-based retrieval methods.
- What the primary source establishes:
  - The study included 2,258 immune-checkpoint-inhibitor patients and 1,426 transcatheter-aortic-valve-replacement patients across Mayo sites in Minnesota, Arizona, and Florida: 3,684 patients total.
  - The Mayo Data Explorer workflow used Gemini 1.5 in the Mayo secure cloud. MeSH-derived terms retrieved potentially relevant documents, and the zero-shot model answered structured yes/no/date questions. Manual clinician adjudication was the reference standard.
  - The LLM achieved the highest AUC for 7 of 8 outcome-cohort combinations. In the cancer cohort, AUCs were 0.920 for stroke, 0.938 for myocardial infarction, 0.873 for heart failure, and 0.880 for composite MACE. ICD retrieval was slightly higher for heart failure at 0.882.
  - In the TAVR cohort, LLM AUCs were 0.915 for stroke, 0.928 for myocardial infarction, 0.844 for heart failure, and 0.862 for MACE. Improvement over ICD retrieval was statistically significant for stroke and MACE, with net reclassification improvements of 25.6% and 12.3%.
  - Internal output inconsistencies occurred in less than 1% of queries and were classified as negative under prespecified rules.
- Why it matters: the study validates outcome ascertainment rather than general clinical question answering. It also shows how narrative-text retrieval and established ICD methods can be selected or combined according to the outcome and cohort.
- Evidence limits: all sites were within one health system. Each chart was reviewed once, so inter-rater agreement was not measured. The evaluation used one institutionally configured model version and no semantic retrieval or fine-tuning. Hybrid methods and formal timing were not tested.
- Follow-up: independent health systems; hybrid LLM-plus-code ascertainment; formal time and cost comparisons; model-version reproducibility; duplicated manual review; and effect-estimate sensitivity to automated outcome definitions.

## What changed

The strongest new systems are no longer asking one model to make an opaque final decision. They divide evidence work into graph constraints, structured agent steps, cascade review, secure text retrieval, prespecified post-processing, and expert adjudication. This makes scale an engineering property of the workflow rather than a claim about model intelligence alone.
